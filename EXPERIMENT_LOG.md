# Experiment log: predicting stellarator equilibrium metrics from boundary Fourier coefficients

A running record of the preprocessing pipeline, training methodology, and architecture search — condensed to conclusions and numbers, not the full blow-by-blow. Full history is in git if needed.

**Dataset** proxima-fusion/constellaration &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Task** 92-dim → 11-target regression &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Rows** 158,685 (of 182,222 raw) &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Compute** Docker + single RTX 5000 Ada

---

## Where things stand

> **⚠ The random 80/10/10 row split overstates generalization, substantially (§6).** 76% of test rows share a generation-lineage family with a training row, and holding out entire *regions* of coefficient space (a cluster split) costs 2.5-3x the aggregate test RMSE versus the random split — some targets 9-19x worse. **Worse: the "best" architecture/optimizer combo found under the random split (wide MLP + SOAP + ensemble) does not clearly win under the cluster split** — it loses to the plain 230K-param baseline on 7 of 11 individual targets, despite being ~4% better in aggregate mean, and its ensemble-disagreement calibration also degrades. Every RMSE number from before §6 was measured under the leaky split; treat all of it as an optimistic upper bound on true generalization, and treat *relative* architecture/optimizer rankings (SIREN vs. MLP, SOAP vs. Adam) as more trustworthy than the absolute numbers, since the same leakage affects everything compared under that split.
>
> **Small-capacity architecture search (random split).** A plain 3-layer MLP (230K params) is a strong baseline. SIREN activation wins two targets by a confirmed noise-floor margin (`min_norm_grad_scale_length` ~7σ, `average_triangularity` ~3σ) at the cost of `aspect_ratio` (~4σ worse) — but this does not survive scaling to 8x width, where plain MLP + SOAP beats SIREN's own wins outright (random split only — unconfirmed under cluster split).
>
> **Normalization (§7): input normalization hurts slightly (~7-9% worse); target z-scoring is roughly neutral in aggregate but meaningfully helps the two worst-generalizing targets (rotational transform, ~45-49% better) under the cluster split.** Worth adopting for those specific targets; not a fix for the generalization gap itself.
>
> **Design-search tool exists** (`scripts/optimize.py`) but was built and tuned entirely against the leaky-split ensemble — its real capability to find genuinely novel (not near-training-data) designs is unverified and, given §6, likely more limited than it looked.
>
> **Confirmed on a real design, not just a held-out split (§8): a design-search "feasible" candidate is not actually feasible.** VMEC++ (pip-installable, low-fidelity mode ~6-14s/call, the same solver that generated this dataset) is now integrated as a ground-truth oracle. Checked against a real `optimize.py` output: the design does physically exist (VMEC++ converges) but **2 of its 3 constraints are violated in reality** (`average_triangularity` −0.374 vs. required ≤−0.5; `edge_rotational_transform` 0.242 vs. required ≥0.3), and `qi` is off by 327%, despite the surrogate ensemble reporting low disagreement at that point. The oracle is fast enough (no hour-long wait) to check every future design-search candidate before trusting it.
>
> **First data-augmentation pilot done and retrained on (§9): 476 new oracle-validated designs, mixed result.** A VAE trained on the real dataset, sampled toward the under-covered clusters identified in §6 (93.9% of generated designs landed nearest an under-covered cluster, vs. ~50% baseline), validated through VMEC++ (~69% hit rate, 15 minutes for 476). Retraining the small baseline on original + 418 of these (train-cluster-anchored only, to avoid leaking the held-out val/test regions back in) **improved 6 of 11 targets — including the two hit hardest by the original gap, `axis_rot/nfp` −26% and `edge_rot/nfp` −35%, the same two targets §7's normalization fix also singled out — but made 5 others worse (`edge_magnetic_mirror_ratio` +70%, `aspect_ratio` +37%), moving the aggregate mean the wrong way (+9%).** Single seed, tiny perturbation (0.3% of training rows) — a real, specific lead worth confirming and scaling up, not yet a settled win.
>
> **Scaled up generation 60x with a different goal: physical-realism coverage, not metric accuracy (§9 cont'd).** Redirected toward broad, untargeted sampling for a follow-up use case that cares about the feasible region's breadth, not hitting specific target values. Two real bugs caught first: fully independent per-coefficient sampling (the most literal "no prior") hit **0% valid over 2,592 attempts** (real boundaries need cross-coefficient correlation the VAE provides); and the very first attempt at broad sampling hung for **2 hours** on a single pathological candidate, since `ProcessPoolExecutor` can't reclaim a stuck worker — fixed with a per-candidate subprocess + hard 45s kill-on-timeout. Landed on unconditional VAE-prior sampling (no anchor, no cluster bias) and ran it for 12 hours: **29,243 validated designs** (18.4% of the original dataset's size), 55.2% hit rate, reaching genuinely beyond the dataset's range on some targets (`aspect_ratio` up to 31.7 vs. the original data's max of 12.1).
>
> **Retrained on the full 29,243-design batch — part of the §9 pilot's pattern is real and strengthens with scale, part of it doesn't replicate.** 24,589 of the 29,243 (the rest anchored in val/test clusters, dropped) added to the cluster-split train set — an 18.5% increase, vs. the pilot's 0.3%. Two targets show a clean, monotonic trend across both sample sizes in the *same* direction — `axis_rot/nfp` −26%→**−49%** and `edge_rot/nfp` −35%→**−49%**, both improving substantially more with more data — and two others worsen further in the same way (`edge_magnetic_mirror_ratio` +70%→**+81%**, `axis_magnetic_mirror_ratio` +32%→**+65%**). That's four targets moving the same direction at two independent sample sizes — real signal, not noise. But three others don't replicate cleanly: `aspect_ratio`'s harm *shrank* (+37%→**+26%**) rather than grew, and `max_elongation`/`average_triangularity` flipped from a pilot-stage improvement to roughly flat-to-slightly-worse (−14%→**+1%**, −22%→**+4%**) — the pilot's apparent win on those two looks like it was mostly small-sample noise. Net: the aggregate mean got worse at the larger scale, not better (+9%→**+15%** over the 9 targets with a logged baseline) — more data amplified the real, specific, already-identified mechanism (rotational transform helped, mirror ratios hurt) without fixing or even clearly worsening the rest. Not a case for blind augmentation; a case for the rotational-transform-specific mechanism being worth pursuing on its own.
>
> **Built a self-training ("bootstrap") loop** (`scripts/bootstrap_loop.py`): each generation retrains the VAE on real + all synthetic accepted so far (warm-started from the previous generation, not from scratch), samples a new batch from it, validates through VMEC++, and appends accepted *and* flagged-rejected designs to a growing pool — with two anti-collapse guards (inflating latent sampling variance each generation, a fixed 10% real-data-only anchor). Run for 30 generations total (§9-10, one power-outage interruption, auto-resumed cleanly): pool now at **92,229 designs**, and since gen26, 20% of every batch is generated by gradient-descending toward minimizing `max_elongation` through the surrogate + VAE decoder (93-95% hit rate on that sub-batch, vs. 15-30% for the rest).
>
> **Gradient-guided search reached a design competitive with the paper's own benchmark (§10).** A second, standalone script (`scripts/gradient_walk.py`) runs persistent latent-space walks, validating every proposed step through real VMEC++ every round (not just at the end). Full run (`walk1`, 28 walks × 50 rounds, target `max_elongation`): **1,270 VMEC++-accepted designs, best measured 1.327** — vs. the ConStellaration paper's reported 1.27, and far past §5's design-search tool (5.88) on the same target. Not yet checked against the paper's other constraints (open thread) — the win is on `max_elongation` alone so far.
>
> **Fresh retrain on the full post-5-day-run pool beat the long run's own final result, and a joint-VAE architecture idea was tried and confirmed worse (§12).** A clean, non-warm-started VAE + production surrogate ensemble retrained on real + all 294,134 accepted synthetic designs found a really-feasible design search candidate at **`max_elongation=2.809`, a 22% improvement over the 5-day run's final block (3.585)**. Separately tested whether routing the metrics-predicting head through a VAE latent trained *jointly* with reconstruction (one shared encoder, two projection heads, not a frozen pretrained encoder) would beat the standard raw-coefficient surrogate — it didn't: **worse on 10 of 11 targets, seed-confirmed (3 seeds each), +33% RMSE on `max_elongation` itself, +10.4% worse in aggregate mean.** 100 further surrogate-feasible (not VMEC++-validated) candidates generated for the same task, saved to `output/generated_candidates_unvalidated.jsonl`, awaiting a real check.
>
> **A second equilibrium code (DESC) needed a basis fix before it agreed with VMEC++ (§14).** Three campaigns produced ι ≈ 0 on nearly every row — a VMEC→DESC conversion that copied coefficients into DESC's product basis directly, dropping the `sin·sin` partner of every mode and with it the helical content that generates rotational transform. More resolution made it *worse*, which is what gave it away. With the explicit trig-identity map, physical ι went from 33/308 to **306/308**, and the two codes now correlate at **0.870** on |ι| (median difference 8.6%), with a ~23% tail still disagreeing by >20%.

## 1. Data & methodology

Everything runs in Docker (never on host); `preprocess`/`train` services share volume-mounted `./output`/`./checkpoints`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Raw rows | 182,222 → kept 158,685 (dropped malformed boundary + invalid targets) |
| Input dim | 92 = `r_cos`(45) + `z_sin`(45) + `n_field_periods`(1) + `is_stellarator_symmetric`(1, always 1.0) |
| Output dim | 11 (of 12 raw — `aspect_ratio_over_edge_rotational_transform` dropped, algebraically redundant and blows up near-zero denominators, std 9,882) |
| Default split | 80/10/10 random rows, seed 42 (see §6 for why this is now flagged) |

**No target normalization** (until §7): raw physical units + learned per-task uncertainty weighting (Kendall & Gal 2018, `loss = Σ exp(-log_var_k)·MSE_k + log_var_k`) instead of z-scoring — each target's own `log_var` is learned, no hand-picked constant, and it's what let a pathological target (the dropped column above) reveal itself as unlearnable rather than just unstable. **No input normalization** (until §7): inputs already zero-mean, O(0.01-0.4) per `metadata.json`. Both re-tested in §7.

Other standing conventions: validate every 5-20 epochs not every epoch; best-val-loss checkpoint (not final epoch) evaluated once against held-out test; matched epoch budgets across compared variants (300, or fewer once SOAP made convergence faster); noise floor measured via seed reruns before trusting any "win" (§2).

## 2. Small-capacity architecture search (300 epochs, random split) — noise floor confirmed

Reran `single_base` 3x (seeds only) to get real run-to-run std per target before trusting any claimed win.

| variant | params | qi | vacuum_well | aspect_ratio | max_elong. | avg_triang. | min_norm_grad |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| single_base (MLP) | 230,550 | 0.00659 | 0.01653 | 0.08390 | 0.43205 | 0.03759 | 0.73323 |
| siren_base | 230,550 | 0.00639 | 0.01846 | 0.09816 | 0.43429 | **0.03243** | **0.68329** |

Confirmed against 3-seed noise floor: SIREN wins `min_norm_grad_scale_length` (~7σ) and `average_triangularity` (~3σ), loses `aspect_ratio` (~4σ worse) — a genuine trade, not a free win, at this capacity only (see §5, doesn't survive scale-up).

Everything else tried at this capacity — dual-path spatial CNN branch, deeper per-target heads, chained sine/ReLU blocks, mode-attention encoder, derived geometric features, log-space target/latent heads, symlog augmentation — either didn't beat the baseline once epoch budgets were matched, or wasn't confirmed beyond noise. Attention was worst on every target (untested at scale/tuning). Log-space heads specifically backfired on their own targets (`min_norm_grad` ~10σ worse, `qi` ~7σ worse) — log-space training optimizes relative/tail accuracy while the eval metric is physical-space RMSE, dominated by bulk accuracy.

## 3. Contrastive framing: pairwise better/worse instead of absolute value

For a surrogate meant to guide design search, whether config A beats config B may matter more than either's exact value. Same backbone, but the loss becomes pairwise comparisons: score diff `d = s_i − s_j` maps through a Davidson (1970) pairwise-with-ties model, `P(i>j) = e^{d/2}/(2cosh(d/2)+ν)`, `P(tie) = ν/(2cosh(d/2)+ν)`, with a learned per-target tie-propensity `ν_k` (the contrastive analogue of `log_var`). Ground-truth "tie" = a pairwise difference smaller than that target's measured noise floor (§2).

Training directly on this beat scoring a regression checkpoint at test time (mean concordance .967 vs .959, MLP) — but naive per-pair-mean training made `ν_k` collapse to "never predict tie" on 10/11 targets, since true tie-rate spans 0.07%-8.4% across targets and rare-tie targets get essentially no gradient signal. Fixed with **per-target adaptive class-balance weighting** (`--tie-weight-cap`, caps the effective majority:minority loss-weight ratio per target rather than using one flat correction strength):

| variant | mean concordance | mean tie-recall | mean tie-precision | targets w/ real tie signal |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| unweighted | .967 | .089 | .068 | 1/11 |
| full class-balance | .872 | .899 | .075 | 11/11, concordance wrecked |
| flat interpolation | .963 | .178 | .092 | 4/11 |
| **per-target adaptive (used)** | **.945** | **.538** | **.101** | **10/11** |

`minimum_normalized_magnetic_gradient_scale_length` never picked up tie signal under any scheme — consistent with it being the hardest, most heavy-tailed target throughout (not a reweighting artifact). SIREN's regression-side edge on `average_triangularity` does not show up once trained directly on the ranking objective (MLP tracks equal-or-ahead everywhere, single seed).

## 4. Scale-up: wide MLP + SOAP ensemble (random split)

Benchmarked latency before scaling rather than assuming: 230K-param model is 0.33ms/sample; even a 112M-param model is only ~1ms. Latency was never the constraint — the real ceiling is dataset size (127K rows), so capacity was spent on **ensembling** (real accuracy + free calibrated uncertainty) rather than blind width.

**8x-wide single-path MLP (7.1M params) + 3-seed ensemble** beat every 230K variant on every target, including erasing SIREN's two confirmed wins. The identical width bump on the **SIREN** trunk regressed on *every* target vs. its own 230K baseline — plausibly `SineLayer`'s `omega_0`/init scheme not transferring to 8x width (untested fix). Ensemble inter-member disagreement vs. actual error was monotonic on 11/11 targets — a real, usable "trust this prediction" signal (**at random-split evaluation only — see §6, this degrades under cluster split**).

**Optimizer: SOAP** (Shampoo-preconditioned Adam) beat Adam on every target by 4-42%, but cost ~10x more wall-clock per epoch — profiled and found the cost was an every-step O(dim³) outer-product update on the wide axes, not just the periodic eigh/QR refresh. Fixed via `--soap-max-precond-dim` (default 1024, skip preconditioning axes wider than this) + `--soap-precondition-frequency` (default 50): **4.5x speedup** (~18.5s → ~4.3s/epoch), also letting the crash-workaround `magma` linalg backend (needed only for the ≥2048-wide case) drop back to faster default cusolver. This fast recipe was carried over to retrain the contrastive objective (§3) on the same wide backbone — concordance/recall/precision all improved together (.945→.950, .538→.579, .101→.112), no tradeoff this time.

## 5. Design search against the frozen surrogate

`scripts/optimize.py`: treats the 90 free Fourier coefficients as the optimization variable, runs Adam directly against the frozen ensemble (backprop through the network, weights fixed) to minimize/maximize one target subject to inequality constraints on others. `n_field_periods` fixed per run (integer, real design variable but the model only saw integers); `is_stellarator_symmetric` fixed at 1.0.

**Caught two real exploits before trusting any result**, both the same underlying failure mode (optimizer finds a corner of coefficient space that satisfies every per-coefficient marginal bound but sits nowhere near the real, correlated data manifold, where the surrogate is unreliable):
1. Per-coefficient clamping alone → predicted `aspect_ratio = −0.64` (physically impossible). Fixed with a **nearest-real-neighbor distance penalty** (squared distance, std-normalized, to the nearest actual same-nfp training point, recomputed every step).
2. Later, adding a proper ALM (augmented-Lagrangian, per-constraint penalty + Lagrange multiplier, ported from the ConStellaration paper's own ALM-NGOpt baseline) let the growing penalty `rho` outpace the *fixed*-weight distance/trust penalties → same failure mode recurred (distance 52.6 vs. normal 0.4-1.1, another impossible prediction). Fixed by **scaling the trust/distance weights together with `rho`**, keeping their relative balance constant regardless of how hard the ALM pushes on real constraints.

Set up a directly-comparable instance (ConStellaration's "Geometric problem": minimize `max_elongation` s.t. `aspect_ratio<=4.0`, `average_triangularity<=-0.5`, `|edge_rotational_transform_over_n_field_periods|>=0.3`, confirmed from their actual source code, not the paper prose). Their best baseline reaches `max_elongation=1.27`; two of their three baselines find *zero* feasible points at all (only ~41/160k dataset points are feasible — a genuinely sparse region). After both exploit fixes: 1/128 starts feasible, `max_elongation=5.88` — real progress (0→1 feasible) but well short of their result, and not yet stress-tested across more objective/constraint combinations.

## 6. Does the random split overstate generalization? Yes, substantially.

Motivating concern: rows aren't independently generated — they come from optimization runs seeded toward specific targets, and one run can produce multiple saved boundaries (continuation iterates, perturbations). Checked the raw dataset directly (not just our preprocessed columns): `omnigenous_field_and_targets.id` (the target spec `T=(ι*,A*,E*,O*)` a run was seeded toward) has **72,536 unique values over 182,222 raw rows — 60% duplication**, group sizes 1-7. Added to `scripts/preprocess.py` as `family_ids.json`.

**Measured leakage directly under the existing random split**: 76% of test rows share a family id with a training row. Nearest-neighbor distance confirms this is geometric, not just metadata: 36.2% of family-matched test rows have a near-exact train duplicate (std-normalized distance < 0.01); even *non*-matched test rows show 16.5% — family-id sharing only explains part of the near-duplication.

Built three split variants (`scripts/make_splits.py`, `train.py --split {random,group,cluster}`): **random** (existing default, reproduced exactly), **group** (whole `family_id` groups assigned to one split, fixes identified lineage leakage), **cluster** (k-means k=100 over std-normalized coefficients, whole clusters assigned to one split — tests generalization to unseen *regions*, the harder and more relevant test given the surrogate's purpose).

**Small baseline (230K MLP, Adam, 300ep) retrained under all three:**

| target | random | group | cluster | cluster vs. random |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| qi | .00717 | .00632 | .01926 | 2.7x |
| aspect_ratio | .06814 | .08220 | .44182 | 6.5x |
| axis_rot/nfp | .01198 | .01311 | .11450 | 9.6x |
| edge_rot/nfp | .01032 | .01039 | .12763 | 12.4x |
| **mean (all 11)** | **.1243** | .1322 (+6%) | **.3141 (+153%)** | |

Group split: only ~6% worse in aggregate (real leakage, modest aggregate effect). Cluster split: 2.5x worse, with rotational-transform targets 9-12x worse — best checkpoint at epoch 20/300 vs. ~220 for the others, i.e. it stopped generalizing almost immediately once asked to extrapolate rather than interpolate.

**Production recipe (wide MLP + SOAP, 3-seed ensemble) retrained under cluster split too** — and the "best" architecture doesn't clearly win anymore:

| | random split mean | cluster split mean | ratio |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| small baseline | .1243 | .3141 | 2.5x |
| production ensemble | .1018 | .3023 | **2.97x** |

Production is only ~4% better than the small baseline in aggregate under cluster split, and **loses outright on 7 of 11 individual targets** (qi, vacuum_well, both mirror ratios, both rotational-transform, min_norm_grad) — despite being the clean, uniform winner on literally every target under the random split. Ensemble calibration also degrades: 3/11 targets non-monotonic (spread vs. error) vs. 1/11 under random split.

**Read:** brute-force width winning cleanly, architecture mattering little, and the nearest-neighbor distance penalty being load-bearing in the design search (§5) are all consistent with a model that's largely doing sophisticated interpolation over a dense, redundant point cloud rather than learning something that extrapolates. None of the *relative* comparisons above (SIREN vs. MLP, SOAP vs. Adam, contrastive reweighting) are necessarily wrong as rankings — the same leakage affects everything compared under the random split — but no absolute RMSE number in §1-5 should be read as true generalization to a genuinely new stellarator family.

## 7. Input/output normalization, re-tested under both splits

Re-tested the §1 "no normalization" decision now that a harder, more honest eval (cluster split) exists — small baseline (230K MLP, Adam, 300ep), both splits:

| variant | random split mean | cluster split mean |
|---|--:|--:|
| baseline (no norm) | .1243 | .3141 |
| normalize-targets (z-score + plain MSE, no uncertainty weighting) | .1246 | **.2941 (−6.4%)** |
| normalize-inputs (standardize 92 features) | .1328 (+6.8%) | .3423 (+9%) |
| both | .1278 (+2.8%) | .3228 (+2.8%) |

**Input normalization hurts slightly, consistently, on both splits** — confirms the original "nothing to normalize" reasoning was right, not just untested. **Target z-scoring is roughly neutral in aggregate but not uniform per-target**: under the cluster split it specifically helps the two targets hit hardest by the generalization gap — `axis_rot/nfp` (.1145→.0629, −45%) and `edge_rot/nfp` (.1276→.0652, −49%) — while modestly hurting `axis_magnetic_mirror_ratio` and `min_norm_grad_scale_length` (+2-4%). Worth adopting per-target rather than globally; not a fix for the generalization gap itself (−6% vs. the 2.5-3x gap it's being measured against).

`--normalize-inputs`/`--normalize-targets` in `train.py`: stats computed from the TRAIN split only (no leakage into val/test), stored in the checkpoint, target predictions always un-normalized back to physical units before reporting — so RMSE stays comparable to every other number in this log regardless of what space a given model trained in.

## 8. VMEC++ as a ground-truth physics oracle

Motivation: §6 showed the surrogate is largely interpolating known configuration families, not extrapolating. The only way to actually validate (or generate data beyond) the training distribution is to check candidates against real physics, not just the surrogate.

**VMEC++ (not STELLOPT).** The ConStellaration dataset itself was generated with VMEC++ — a modern reimplementation of VMEC by the same team, pip-installable, no MPI/GPU required, with the exact same boundary Fourier representation and target metric names as this project's data. `constellaration.forward_model.forward_model(boundary, settings=...)` wraps it directly; `settings=None` defaults to **low fidelity** (what generated our dataset), `ConstellarationSettings.default_high_fidelity()` for later dialing-in on promising candidates. STELLOPT (Fortran, MPI, much heavier to integrate) was not needed.

**Docker image change, one real bug caught and fixed.** `pip install vmecpp constellaration` needed `libnetcdf-dev` + `build-essential`/`gfortran`/`ninja-build` added to `Dockerfile.train` (for `booz-xform`, a C++ extension dependency, previously entirely absent from the image). That install also silently upgraded numpy 1.26→2.2 as a resolved dependency, which **broke `torch.from_numpy` outright** (`RuntimeError: Numpy is not available` — torch 2.2.0's C extensions are compiled against numpy's 1.x ABI). Caught via an actual training smoke test, not just an import check. Fixed by pinning `numpy<2` after the physics packages install; verified empirically that `constellaration`'s declared `numpy>=2.1.3` requirement is conservative, not a real hard floor — it imports and runs correctly against 1.26.4.

**Validated the oracle against known ground truth first.** Reconstructed a real dataset row as a `SurfaceRZFourier` boundary, ran it through `forward_model` at low fidelity: recomputed metrics matched the dataset's stored values closely (most within 2%, worst was `min_norm_grad_scale_length` at ~6%) — confirms correct integration, not just "it runs." **Took 5.7s low-fidelity**, not an hour. An invalid/unphysical boundary fails in ~0.02s with a clear error (either a pydantic structural-validation error, or a fast VMEC++ solver `RuntimeError`) rather than hanging — a free, cheap "is this even physically possible" signal, exactly the fast-check-before-dialing-in workflow wanted. Built as `scripts/oracle_eval.py` (`--row-index N` for dataset sanity checks, `--design-json PATH` for `optimize.py --save` outputs, `--fidelity {low,high}`).

**Ran the actual design-search output through it — confirms the §6 concern directly, on a real optimizer-produced design, not just a held-out split.** The `optimize.py` ConStellaration geometric-problem candidate (§5, reported "1/128 feasible," `max_elongation=5.88`, low ensemble disagreement at the time) was fed to the real oracle. VMEC++ converges — the design **is** physically buildable — but:

| target | surrogate predicted | VMEC++ ground truth | rel. diff |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| aspect_ratio (constraint ≤4.0) | 4.018 | 4.080 | 1.5% |
| max_elongation (the objective) | 5.882 | 5.174 | −12.0% |
| average_triangularity (constraint ≤−0.5) | −0.506 | **−0.374** | 26.0% — **constraint actually violated** |
| edge_rot/nfp (constraint ≥0.3) | 0.330 | **0.242** | −26.8% — **constraint actually violated** |
| qi | 0.0357 | **0.152** | **327%** |
| min_norm_grad | 1.972 | 0.392 | −80.1% |
| vacuum_well | −0.602 | −0.551 | 8.5% |

**The design the surrogate reported as feasible is not actually feasible** — 2 of 3 constraints fail against real physics, despite low ensemble disagreement at the reported optimum. This is the concrete, real-design confirmation of §6's split-based finding: the design-search tool as built cannot yet be trusted to certify feasibility on its own; every candidate needs oracle verification before it means anything.

## 9. Generative pilot: augmenting the dataset toward a fuller manifold

Goal, directly motivated by §6: stop being bound to the existing dataset's coverage. First pass at generating *new*, oracle-validated designs concentrated in the regions the dataset covers most thinly, rather than more of the same.

**Pipeline:** conditional VAE (`scripts/train_vae.py`) over the 90 free boundary coefficients, conditioned on `n_field_periods`, trained on the *full* real dataset (158,685 rows — this model's job is to know the real manifold as well as possible, not to generalize the way the surrogate is evaluated). Its only purpose is to raise the hit rate against VMEC++ above what sampling raw noise would achieve — real boundaries are highly structured (9 of 90 coefficients are exactly zero by symmetry, others tightly correlated), so undirected sampling would rarely produce anything that converges at all.

**Sampling biased toward under-covered regions** (`scripts/generate_and_validate.py`): reused §6's k-means cluster assignment (now persisted as `cluster_assignments.npy`/`cluster_sizes.json`) to identify the 50 smallest-by-row-count of the 100 clusters (≤1,334 rows, vs. up to 10,025 for the largest) as "under-covered." Each candidate: pick a random real row from an under-covered cluster as an anchor, encode it to its VAE latent mean, decode a nearby point (anchor + Gaussian noise, `--explore-std`) using the anchor's own `n_field_periods` — concentrates new samples where the dataset is thin without discarding the structure the VAE learned.

**Validated through the real VMEC++ oracle (§8) in parallel across 28 of the machine's 32 CPU cores** (`ProcessPoolExecutor`, low fidelity) — every candidate either converges (kept, with VMEC++'s own computed metrics as ground-truth labels) or fails fast, incrementally checkpointed to `output/generated/{X,Y}.npy` (same format as the original dataset) so a long run can't lose progress.

**Pilot run: 476 validated designs in 15 minutes**, ~69% hit rate (0 structural rejections, all rejections were genuine VMEC++ convergence failures — the VAE never produced a topologically-invalid candidate). Confirmed the targeting actually worked: **93.9% of generated designs have their nearest real neighbor in an under-covered cluster**, vs. the ~50% baseline that untargeted sampling would give. Target ranges stayed physically sensible and mostly within the real dataset's range — with `aspect_ratio` reaching 16.4, genuinely *beyond* the real dataset's max of 12.1, a first concrete sign of stretching past the existing manifold rather than just resampling within it.

Not yet done: retraining the surrogate on the augmented dataset to see if this actually narrows the §6 generalization gap — this pilot only builds and validates the augmentation pipeline and produces the first batch of new data.

**Redirected: scaled-up generation for physical-realism coverage, not metric accuracy.** Explicit change in what this is for — the follow-up use case cares about breadth of the *feasible region* itself (VMEC++ convergence as a binary signal), not matching specific target values, and isn't meant to feed a full active-learning loop yet. Two changes from the pilot: dropped cluster-targeting in favor of broad, untargeted sampling ("basically no prior"), and this needed two real fixes before it could run unattended for hours.

**First fix attempt failed outright and revealed something important.** Tried fully independent per-coefficient uniform sampling (each of the 81 free coefficients sampled uniformly within its own observed range, no VAE at all) as the most literal "no prior." **0% hit rate over 2,592 VMEC++ attempts.** Real boundaries have strong cross-coefficient correlations (smoothness, non-self-intersection) that sampling each coefficient independently destroys completely — confirms the VAE isn't just a convenience, it's load-bearing for getting *any* usable hit rate at all once cluster-targeting is removed.

**Second bug, caught the hard way: VMEC++ can hang instead of failing fast.** The very first attempt at this (before the fix above was even tried) sat running for **2 hours** on a target of 20 candidates before being killed manually — a pathological low-quality random geometry made the solver spin instead of erroring out the way the clearly-invalid cases in §8/§9 did. `ProcessPoolExecutor` has no way to reclaim a worker stuck on one non-terminating task. Replaced it with a per-candidate throwaway subprocess + hard wall-clock timeout (45s, terminate then SIGKILL) — confirmed working in testing (caught and killed a hung candidate mid-run without stalling anything else).

**Landed on unconditional VAE-prior sampling** (`--sampling-mode vae-prior`): `z ~ N(0,1)` in the VAE's latent space, no anchor, no cluster bias, `n_field_periods` uniform over {1..5} — enough real correlational structure to actually work, but not targeted at any particular region the way §9's pilot was. Quick test: 62.5% hit rate. Kicked off a 12-hour run on this basis.

**12-hour run result: 29,243 validated designs** (18.4% the size of the entire original 158,685-row dataset) from 52,976 attempts, 55.2% hit rate, 0 structural rejections, 4,141 timeouts correctly caught and killed. Clean data, no NaNs. Real reach beyond the original dataset on some targets: generated `aspect_ratio` hit 31.7 (real dataset max: 12.1), `qi` hit 0.30 (real max: 0.27). As expected for untargeted sampling, coverage skewed toward the already-dense clusters (a 3,000-point subsample: only 8.7% landed nearest an under-covered cluster, vs. 93.9% for §9's targeted pilot) — but still touched 73+ of the 100 clusters.

**Retrained on all 24,589 train-cluster-anchored designs from this batch (60x the pilot's scale): confirms part of the pilot's pattern, doesn't replicate the rest.** `axis_rot/nfp`/`edge_rot/nfp` improved substantially more with more data (−26%→−49%, −35%→−49% vs. no-augmentation baseline) and the mirror-ratio targets got substantially more worse (+32%→+65%, +70%→+81%) — four targets, two independent sample sizes, same direction each time, real signal. But `aspect_ratio`'s harm shrank instead of growing (+37%→+26%), and `max_elongation`/`average_triangularity` flipped from a pilot-stage improvement to roughly flat (−14%→+1%, −22%→+4%) — those two looked like small-sample noise, not a real effect. Aggregate mean got worse at scale, not better (+9%→+15% over the 9 targets with a logged baseline). Reads as: a specific, real, worth-pursuing mechanism on rotational-transform targets, not a case for augmenting blindly.

**Built `scripts/bootstrap_loop.py`** for a self-training loop per this session's follow-up request: each generation warm-starts the VAE on real + all synthetic accepted so far, samples a new batch (10% from the original real-data-only VAE as a fixed anti-collapse anchor, 10% pure independent-per-coefficient random for zero-learned-bias negative examples, 80% from the current generation with inflating latent variance `std_g = 1 + 0.10g`), validates through VMEC++, and appends accepted + reason-flagged-rejected designs to a growing pool with a per-generation coverage/range-extension log. `generate_and_validate.py` also now persists rejected candidates (structural/vmec/timeout/**unknown** — worker crash or died-without-result, kept distinct from a confirmed VMEC physics rejection) going forward, rather than discarding them; every attempted candidate now ends up in either the accepted or rejected pool, nothing thrown away.

**Ran it for 15 generations (~30h total) — confirms the anti-collapse guards work, and confirms std growth is the actual coverage driver, not just retraining.** Generations 1-10 (std growing 1.10→2.00): coverage rose monotonically the whole way, 6.8%→20.2%, hit rate declined gradually and gracefully (45.3%→20.4%, not a collapse), pool reached 34,400 designs, range-extension breadth grew from 2 targets to 6 (`qi`, `vacuum_well`, `aspect_ratio`, `edge_magnetic_mirror_ratio`, `axis_magnetic_mirror_ratio`, `minimum_normalized_magnetic_gradient_scale_length`). Added a `--max-std` cap (generation count and exploration radius were previously hard-tied — no way to keep running generations without also pushing std wider each time) and ran 5 more generations frozen at std=2.0: hit rate **stabilized** (19.7-21.7%, no further decline — confirms std growth, not compounding retraining instability, was what drove the earlier decline), but coverage-fraction and range-extension both **plateaued** (coverage oscillating 18.3-21.6% with no clear trend; the same 6 targets showing extension, magnitudes creeping only marginally). Pool still grew substantially in raw volume (34,400→47,776) at a stable hit rate. Reads as: std growth is the one lever that expands reach; more generations at a fixed std mostly consolidate/add volume at the existing frontier rather than push further. To keep extending coverage, std needs to keep growing (at some rate); to hold quality/hit-rate steady, cap it — those are in tension, not a bug to fix.

## 10. Gradient-guided search for low `max_elongation`: directed sampling, then a dedicated latent-space walk

**The bootstrap loop kept running past §9's last logged point (gen15, 47,776-design pool) through gen30 (92,229) — 15 more generations, previously undocumented.** Switched from the flat `--max-std 2.0` cap to a `relax → expand → fill` cycle schedule (`--schedule cycle`), with each cycle's peak std ratcheting up by 0.2 (2.2 → 2.4 → 2.6 across cycles 0-2). Coverage-of-under-covered-clusters stayed flat around 21-23% through gen16-25 (cycles 0-1) — consistent with §9's finding that generations at a fixed-ish std mostly add volume rather than push reach, now confirmed to hold under cycling too, not just a flat cap.

**Gen26 added directed sampling** (`bootstrap_loop.py --directed-frac 0.2`): 20% of every batch is generated by gradient-descending in the current generation's VAE latent space, through the decoder and the frozen surrogate, toward minimizing `max_elongation` (`n_steps=3` per proposal, reusing `gradient_walk.py`'s `load_surrogate`) — then validated through real VMEC++ exactly like every other candidate, no special treatment. Gen26-30: directed sub-batch hit rate **93-95%**, far above the 15-30% the rest of each generation was getting at that point's exploration radius, and the surrogate's own predicted mean tracked the VMEC++-measured mean within a few percent every generation (gen30: 4.199 predicted vs. 4.141 measured) — gradient-following toward a target the surrogate can see holds up much better here than the open-ended optimizer candidate that failed 2/3 unrelated constraints in §8. Pool grew 72,554 → 92,229 over these 5 generations.

**Run died mid-generation-31 in a power outage; resumed cleanly.** `bootstrap_loop.py`'s auto-resume (same `--tag`, matches on the log's last recorded generation/vae-tag/schedule state) picked back up with no data loss — confirmed by inspection, not yet re-run further.

**Built a second, standalone gradient method: `scripts/gradient_walk.py`.** Where the directed-sampling above is one-shot (3 steps, discarded and re-anchored every batch), this runs persistent walks: each of `--n-walks` walkers takes one gradient step per round with one *shared* adaptive step size, decodes and validates every walker's proposed step through real VMEC++ every round (not just at the end), and a walker whose step fails VMEC++ rolls back to its last valid point rather than wandering into infeasible territory; the shared step size shrinks if too few of a round's proposals validate. Smoke-tested (3-4 rounds, 4 walks) first, then run at full scale — **`--tag walk1`, defaults (28 walks, 50 rounds, target `max_elongation`/min, anchored on real designs' encoded latents): 1,270 VMEC++-accepted designs, mean predicted `max_elongation` fell from ~4.8 (round 1) to 2.99 (round 50), best single accepted design measured 1.327** — within range of the ConStellaration paper's own reported optimum of 1.27, and far past what §5's design-search tool reached against the frozen surrogate (5.88). Every accepted point, like every other source in this project, is a real VMEC++ result, not a surrogate prediction.

## 14. DESC as a second equilibrium code: a VMEC→DESC mode-mapping bug

Ran DESC (`desc-opt`) vacuum solves over the same 308 VMEC++-converged boundaries, to have a second, independent equilibrium code to check VMEC++ against. The first three campaigns produced **ι ≈ 0 on nearly every row** — impossible for a vacuum stellarator, where the rotational transform is precisely what the boundary shaping generates.

**More resolution made it worse, which is what identified the cause.** L=M=N=12 (395 s/solve, 8× the L=8 cost) came back with 17/308 physical ι versus 40/308 at L=8, and the near-zero transforms moved an order of magnitude *closer* to zero (median |ι| 6.4e-7 → 7.3e-8). A solve failing to converge does not do that; a solve **converging correctly onto the wrong problem** does. Comparing the saved boundaries against a direct synthesis confirmed it: they differed from the requested shape by **0.06–0.28 m**, against a ~0.13 m minor radius.

**The bug was the basis conversion.** VMEC writes `R = Σ r_cos·cos(mθ − n·NFP·φ)`; DESC uses a *product* of trig functions. One VMEC term therefore expands into **two** DESC modes:

```
cos(mθ − nφ) = cos(mθ)cos(nφ) + sin(mθ)sin(nφ)   ->   modes (m, n) AND (−m, −n)
```

The campaigns copied coefficients into DESC's basis directly, writing only the first and leaving exact zeros in every `(−m, −n)` slot — 32 of 145 R modes and 36 of 144 Z modes. Dropping the `sin·sin` partner destroys the helical excursion of the surface, and the helical excursion is what produces rotational transform. Hence ι ≈ 0, and hence more spectral resolution only resolving that zero more precisely.

The correct conversion is written out explicitly as `vmec_to_desc_modes` (DESC's own `ptolemy_identity_fwd` is the same identity, but its square solve is singular on this project's dense `m=0, ±n` cosine grid, where `cos(−nφ) = cos(nφ)`). It reproduces the boundary to **1e-16**. Rebuilding the campaign through it lifted physical ι from 33/308 to **306/308**.

**Cross-check against VMEC++.** On |ι|, the two codes correlate at **0.870**, median relative difference **8.6%**, within 20% on **238/308** rows. Evaluated at matched flux surfaces the distribution is bimodal rather than uniformly offset: most surfaces agree to **0.1–2%** — genuine mutual confirmation between independent codes — while **70 of 308** rows disagree by >20%. That tail is not explained by the 13 rows with pathological signed geometry (only 3 of the 70). The likeliest remaining cause is DESC settling on a nearby but different equilibrium for strongly-shaped boundaries; its non-nested-initial-guess warning fires on some of them.

DESC's ι comes out with the **opposite sign** to VMEC++. That is a handedness convention (the surface mapping deliberately does not let DESC reorient the parameterization), not a disagreement — compare |ι|, and do not "fix" it by flipping the boundary.

**Open:** the ~23% of rows where the two codes disagree by >20%, the 2 rows with |ι| > 2, and the 13 rows with negative signed volume/aspect/R0.

Consistent with a `ns_array`-style progressive-resolution re-screen (e.g. 25→51→99 flux surfaces, per Connor). Reads as: the 70.4% figure is "how many low-fidelity-accepted designs survive a high-fidelity re-solve," not an error in the original pool.

**Also confirmed: the pool does not contain the intentionally-degenerate negative-example candidates.** Checked `bootstrap_loop.py` directly — `accepted_X`/`X.npy` only receives a row when VMEC++ actually ran successfully *and* every target metric came back finite (lines ~471-491); every other outcome, including the purposely-invalid "10% pure independent-per-coefficient random" sampler (§9, explicitly commented in the code as being "for the rejected-candidate pool as zero-learned-bias negative examples, not for adding accepted designs"), is routed to `rejected_X.npy` with a reason code and never touches `X.npy`. So the 294,134-row pool `file_rows` indexes into is, by construction, only ever-once-passed designs — the 70.4% re-screen figure is new information, not contamination from a set that was never meant to be accepted.

**Reference baselines** (`scripts/screen_reference_baselines.py`, run on host — not Docker, meant as a lightweight floor, not the production recipe; random 80/10/10 split, seed 42, matching the project's original default per §1). Eight models: `ridge`/`logistic regression` (linear floor), `HistGradientBoosting` (sklearn), `xgboost` (GPU, `multi_strategy="multi_output_tree"` for stage 2 — one shared-tree model across all 11 targets instead of 11 independent boosters), `mlp_sklearn` (lbfgs/adam), and four SOAP-optimized (`scripts/soap.py`, arxiv 2409.11321, already the production recipe's optimizer per §2/§4) GPU trunks reused directly from `train.py`'s `build_spectral_trunk` + a plain linear head (no spatial branch, no log-target heads, none of `DualPathMLP`'s other machinery) — `mlp_soap`, `siren_soap` (Sitzmann et al. 2020), `half_siren_soap` (each layer half-sine/half-ReLU, untried here until now), `attention_soap` (per-Fourier-mode self-attention, §13's least-invested/worst-tested variant on the real dataset).

**Two real bugs found and fixed in this pass, both the hard way (direct A/B, not guessed):**

1. **Input scaling is architecture-specific, not a SIREN-only quirk.** SIREN initially looked like the weak point (AUC 0.898 scaled vs. 0.960 raw) — traced to `SineLayer`'s init being calibrated for the coefficients' raw O(0.01-0.4) scale (the same scale §7 already settled on as "no input normalization"), broken by `StandardScaler`. But a direct A/B on `mlp_soap` too found it *also* prefers raw input (AUC 0.9614 raw vs. 0.9494 scaled) — `attention_soap` is the one architecture that actually wants scaled input (0.9611 vs. 0.9576 raw). Fixed per-architecture: `mlp`/`siren`/`half_siren` get raw input, `attention` gets scaled.
2. **Physical-unit RMSE for `max_elongation` (and the other 3 wide-dynamic-range targets) is fundamentally broken for ranking models, even after log-transforming the target for training.** Predicting `log(target)` for train.py's `LOG_TARGET_NAMES` (`qi`, `max_elongation`, `flux_compression_in_regions_of_bad_curvature`, `minimum_normalized_magnetic_gradient_scale_length`) fixed training, but evaluating the exp'd prediction in physical-unit RMSE still didn't differentiate models at all — every one of 8 architectures converged to ~the same `max_elongation` RMSE (3971-3977, ridge and attention_soap within 0.15% of each other). Root cause: **3 of 20,708 test rows** (values 118k/279k/477k) account for ~99% of the total squared error regardless of model — a small log-space miss on an extreme outlier still produces an astronomical physical-space error, so physical RMSE post-exp measures "how badly did you miss these 3 rows," not fit quality. Fixed by evaluating in **eval space** (log-space for the 4 flagged targets, physical units for the other 7) instead — this is the metric the tables below use for ranking; a physical-unit table is also printed for reference but isn't used to compare models.

## 16. Target-space segmentation and a generation-from-target-metrics baseline

§15 built the forward direction (design -> target metrics) split/baselines. Natural next step: the reverse (target metrics -> design), which needs its own held-out-region test (§6's cluster split holds out *input*-space regions; a generation model needs *target*-space regions held out instead) and an actual first baseline model for that direction.

**Target-cluster split** (`make_splits.py --modes target-cluster`): k-means (k=100) over the 11 targets, z-scored, with the 4 wide-dynamic-range targets in `LOG_TARGET_NAMES` log-transformed first (same convention as `train.py`'s log-target heads, for the same reason: raw-space euclidean clustering would otherwise be dominated by a handful of `max_elongation`/`qi` outliers) — same real 158,685-row dataset §6 clustered in input space, mirrored into target space. **127,429 train / 16,862 val / 14,394 test** rows (81/11/8 of the 100 clusters), cluster sizes range 213-3,577 (median 1,539) — comparable spread to §6's input-space clusters. Saved to `output/splits/target_cluster/` plus `target_cluster_assignments.npy`/`target_cluster_sizes.json`/`target_cluster_split_membership.json`, same artifact pattern §6 established.

**Conditional VAE baseline** (`scripts/train_cvae.py`): `train_vae.py`'s architecture, decoder conditioned on the 11 (log+z-scored) targets + nfp one-hot instead of just nfp — a direct, one-shot "design from targets" counterpart to §15's forward baselines, and a fundamentally different approach from `latent_optimize.py`'s iterative gradient search against a frozen surrogate (one decode call vs. an optimization loop per candidate). Trained **only** on `target_cluster`'s train split (127,429 rows), not the full dataset the way `train_vae.py` trains — this model's whole point is to be scored on target regions it never trained near, so training on val/test clusters would invalidate that before it starts. Coefficient and target normalization stats both computed from the train split alone, not `metadata.json`'s full-dataset stats (§7's leakage discipline). 200 epochs, seed 0, 211K params: recon loss 12.6 → 4.68, KL 102 → 71.4, clean monotonic shape, no divergence.

**Evaluated against the real VMEC++ oracle on 40 held-out target-cluster test specs** (`scripts/eval_cvae_generation.py`, k=5 candidates/spec, reusing `generate_and_validate.py`'s subprocess-per-candidate + hard-timeout harness unmodified — same hang risk applies to any candidate it hasn't seen before): **71.0% candidate-level hit rate** (142/200 converged), **97.5% spec-level hit rate** (39/40 specs got ≥1 converged candidate) — close to §9's vae-cluster pilot (69%) despite conditioning on continuous target regions never seen in training. The "produce a plausible boundary at all" skill transfers about as well to held-out target-space regions as it did to held-out input-space regions in §9.

**But precision loses cleanly to the cheapest possible floor.** Best-of-5 median-relative-target-error (eval space: log for the 4 wide-dynamic-range targets, physical units otherwise — §15's ranking convention) across the 39 converged specs: **cVAE 0.3105** vs. **nearest-neighbor retrieval 0.0743** (closest train-split row by the same z-scored target distance the clusters were built from — zero training, zero VMEC++ calls, always feasible by construction since it's a real already-validated design). **Retrieval is ~4.2x more precise** at actually landing on the requested targets. Reading: the cVAE learned "what a plausible boundary looks like" well (high hit rate) but not "how to steer it toward a specific target combination" (poor precision) — the coefficient-manifold prior is dominating the decode over the target conditioning signal. Same shape of concern as §12's joint-VAE ablation (this architecture's bottleneck loses target-relevant information) approached from the opposite direction, and the same caution §8 established for the forward search direction applies here too: a generation baseline has to beat retrieval, not just look feasible, to be worth using going forward.

**Open:**
- Single seed, no noise-floor rerun (§2's standing bar) — the 0.31 vs. 0.07 gap is large but unconfirmed against seed-to-seed training variance.
- k=5/spec, n=40 specs is a cheap first read, not swept — larger k, more specs, or explore-std variants on z would all plausibly move the hit-rate/precision tradeoff.
- No comparison yet against `latent_optimize.py`'s iterative search adapted to an arbitrary target spec (the expensive-but-plausibly-more-precise alternative to this cheap one-shot decode).
- `beta` (KL weight) untuned — a lower beta might let the decoder lean on target conditioning harder, at some hit-rate cost; worth a direct sweep given how one-sided the current hit-rate-vs-precision result is.
- Only the real dataset was clustered/trained on here, per this session's choice — the bootstrap pool (§12, denser target-space coverage per §9-10) is an open alternative once this baseline's precision problem is understood, not before.

## 17. A crisper generation test: validity-at-spot + directional steerability, not retrieval

§16's retrieval comparison answered a question this project doesn't actually care about ("does the generator beat looking up the nearest existing design"). Reframed per direct user request into the two capabilities that matter: (1) can the model produce *valid* designs at an arbitrary requested target spot, and (2) does nudging one target in the conditioning vector move the *oracle-measured* value of that target the requested direction -- a real controllability/gradient-landscape check, not a "did you reproduce the original row" check (never the point). No train/test split needed for either question -- this isn't measuring generalization to an unseen region (that's still §16/`target-cluster`'s job, deferred to a later cluster-wise pass per explicit user request), so `train_cvae.py` gained a `--source full` mode (train on all 158,685 real rows, `train_vae.py`'s own convention) and was retrained as `cvae_targets_full_s0`.

**Built `scripts/eval_cvae_steerability.py`.** Anchors are chosen by farthest-point sampling (greedy max-min distance) in the same z-scored/log-transformed target space the model conditions on, instead of cluster-holdout or plain random draw -- spreads the test across target space without a hard split. At each anchor: 10 validity candidates decoded at the anchor's own target vector, then 11 steerability probes (one per target, each nudging only that target by `--step-std` in eval space, decoded, validated). Also benchmarked VMEC++ fidelity presets directly (`from_boundary_resolution`, VMEC++'s 25->51->99 multigrid, vs. the `low_fidelity` 25->71 preset used everywhere else in this project): ~48s/call vs. ~17s/call on the same real row, both landing on nearly identical `max_elongation` (6.7344 vs 6.7347) for that row.

**Two methodology bugs caught before any result about the model could be trusted, both from direct user pushback, not internal review.**

1. `correct_direction_rate` was first computed against *all* converged steer candidates, including ones whose anchor had no valid baseline to measure a delta against -- silently deflating the stat. Fixed (`steer_by_dim[d]["judgeable"]`, counted only when a baseline exists).

2. **The bigger one.** The first baseline design was a same-fidelity re-verification of the anchor's own *real* row from `X.npy`/`Y.npy`. Only 4-5 of 8 anchors' real designs reconverged under our own oracle at either fidelity, read at the time as "farthest-point sampling finds the fragile edge of the feasible region" -- a plausible-sounding physical story. Directly questioned ("isn't convergence supposed to be deterministic?"), which it is -- confirmed by rerunning one failing row twice: identical error, identical ~1s failure time, both times. The real explanation is upstream of the solver: `preprocess.py` never runs VMEC++ on these rows at all -- `X`/`Y` come straight from the `proxima-fusion/constellaration` HuggingFace dataset's own precomputed `metrics.*` columns (confirmed via the HF dataset API: stored as float64 there), only float32-downcast here for storage. Re-verifying that already-quantized boundary through our own oracle is deterministic, but it isn't reproducing whatever produced the original numbers -- and for our own docs, `low_fidelity` is explicitly documented as **"optimizes for runtime over correctness/fidelity"**, never claimed to reproduce Proxima Fusion's own settings (§8 only ever confirmed *loose*, 2-6%, agreement on a row that did converge). For a design sitting at VMEC's force-tolerance edge (1e-17 at the first multigrid stage), a rounding-level input difference plus a possible settings/version gap is enough to flip convergence outright, deterministically, every time -- not evidence about the anchors' physical fragility at all.

   **Fix: the baseline is now the mean of the anchor's own converged validity candidates** -- decoded by this model, validated by this oracle, at this fidelity. Fully self-consistent, no dependency on reproducing Proxima Fusion's pipeline. The old real-design re-verification is kept only as a labeled diagnostic field, not used in any computation.

   **Directly asked (correctly) whether this meant the training data itself was untrustworthy -- checked with a random, non-cherry-picked sample rather than answering from theory.** 20 rows drawn uniformly from `X.npy` (not farthest-point-selected) through the same local low-fidelity oracle: **19/20 (95%) reconverged cleanly**, times 1.1-12.4s, nothing pathological. The one failure (`JACOBIAN_75_TIMES_BAD`) is a standard, recognized VMEC failure mode for poorly-conditioned geometry, not evidence of a bug. **The reproduction gap found above is concentrated in the deliberately-extreme farthest-point anchors, not a general property of the dataset** -- the training data is not broadly suspect; this section's finding was narrower than "can we trust this data," and should have been checked this way before speculating about mechanism.

   Also worth recording: this project already built a second, independent equilibrium-solver cross-check for exactly this kind of question -- `scripts/vmec_jax_oracle.py`, which ports `constellaration`'s own VMEC++ problem setup (`vmec_utils.build_vmecpp_indata`, not re-derived independently, since an earlier attempt at re-deriving it was itself "a real source of false mismatches") into `vmec_jax` and solves the identical problem with a different solver. Its docstring documents a real, already-fixed bug (`indata.rbc/zbs` are `[m, n+ntor]` in VMEC++'s convention, `[n+ntor, m]` in vmec_jax's dense array -- a transpose, not a reshape) and says "see EXPERIMENT_LOG" for where it was found -- **but no such entry exists anywhere in this file.** That investigation happened (per the file's own commit, predating §14) and was never written up here, only in the code comment. The tool is also currently unrunnable as documented: `Dockerfile.oracle` dropped `vmec-jax` entirely when DESC was added (`vmec-jax` pins `jax==0.10.x`, `desc-opt` requires `jax<0.10` -- a real conflict, not an oversight), so cross-checking against it would need a fresh environment. Given the 95% random-sample agreement above, reviving it isn't a blocker for the current work, but it's a legitimate second-opinion tool this project already paid for once and let bit-rot -- worth reviving if the disagreement tail on marginal designs ever needs closer characterization.

| **nearest-neighbor retrieval** (no generation at all) | **95.5% (126/132)** |

**Reading:** the cVAE clears its own chance-level floor by a real, if modest, margin (~13-16 points) -- the target conditioning is doing *something*, not pure noise, consistent with the earlier read. But it's far short of what trivial retrieval gets in this same dense region of target space (95.5%), echoing §16's original precision finding from a completely different angle and metric. Not a surprising result once stated plainly -- with 158,685 real rows, "find the nearest one to a slightly-shifted target" is an easy lookup, and no generative model should be expected to beat that *in a region the dataset already covers densely*. The cVAE's actual value proposition, if it has one, was never "beat retrieval where retrieval already works" -- it's reaching target combinations retrieval can't reach at all (§9's original motivation for the VAE augmentation pipeline). That's exactly the cluster-wise, held-out-target-region test still deferred below, now clearly the more important next step rather than an optional follow-up.

**Open:**
- The chance-level control itself came in above 50% (54.7%, not ~50%) -- unexplained, worth a look (possibly a mild asymmetry in the decode distribution or in how `baseline_e`, the validity-candidate mean, sits relative to individual samples).
- Single seed throughout every variant here -- worth a seed-rerun before fully trusting the specific numbers, per §2's standing convention.
- `--step-std 1.0` untuned -- unclear whether a smaller, more local step behaves differently, for either the cVAE or the retrieval baseline (a smaller step likely helps retrieval even more, since the nearest neighbor gets closer to the anchor itself).
- The cluster-wise (held-out target-region) version of this test is no longer just "the harder follow-up" -- it's the one place retrieval *can't* trivially win, and therefore the more informative test of whether the cVAE is worth anything beyond what a lookup table already gives.
- `vmec_jax_oracle.py` revival -- optional, not urgent given the 95% agreement, but the right tool if the disagreement tail needs closer study later.
- `--anchor-selection farthest` is still there for a deliberate stress-test pass later -- now correctly understood as testing robustness at the edges of target space, not a fair read of typical capability.

## 18. Diffusion and GAN baselines: is 68-71% correct-direction a data/task limit or a VAE limit?

§17's retrieval comparison was dropped per explicit user pushback: retrieval solves a strictly easier problem ("find the best existing match," never having to synthesize anything) than generation does, so it isn't a fair competitor and "beating" it was never really the right question -- it stayed useful only as a sanity floor, not as *the* baseline. Redirected instead into comparing generative model *families* against each other: does the cVAE's modest steerability reflect something fundamental about this task, or specifically a limitation of the VAE architecture?

**Built `scripts/train_diffusion.py`**: a standard DDPM (Ho et al. 2020) over the same 90 coefficients, same conditioning (11 log+z-scored targets + nfp one-hot), same `--source full` convention, same coefficient-standardization discipline as `train_cvae.py` -- only the generative mechanism differs (linear beta schedule, T=200, simple noise-prediction MSE loss, ancestral DDPM sampling). Denoiser has 206,810 params, comparable to the cVAE's 211,098 total -- not a capacity handicap, matching §12's own standard for a fair architecture comparison.

**Then built `scripts/train_gan.py`**, a third family: conditional WGAN-GP (Gulrajani et al. 2017, gradient-penalty critic rather than a vanilla BCE-loss GAN, specifically for training stability without a tuning campaign this baseline was never going to get). Generator is 101,466 params -- which turns out to match the cVAE decoder's own param count exactly (the cVAE's 211,098-param total includes an encoder that's only ever used for training, never at generation time; 101,466 is what actually runs at sampling time for both). `n_critic=5`, `lr=1e-4`, `betas=(0.5,0.9)` -- standard WGAN-GP defaults, also untuned. Generator loss climbed steadily through all 200 training epochs (14 -> 58) rather than settling -- on its face a bad sign, but WGAN losses aren't sample-quality metrics (they're an estimate of Wasserstein distance under a constantly-shifting critic), so per this project's own standing discipline of not trusting a training curve over the real oracle (§8 onward), it was evaluated anyway rather than discarded on the strength of the loss curve alone.

**`eval_cvae_steerability.py` generalized to `--model-type {cvae,diffusion,gan}`** around one swappable `sample_fn(cond, k)` -- same anchors, same oracle, same fidelity, same null-mode control for all three, so their numbers are directly comparable, not just similarly-produced.

**Full comparison, same 12 random anchors/seed=0 throughout:**

| model | fidelity | validity hit rate | correct-direction (targeted) | correct-direction (null/chance) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| cVAE | low | 55.8% | 67.9% (167/246) | 54.7% (141/258) |
| cVAE | medium | 43.3% | 70.8% (150/212) | -- |
| diffusion | low | 69.2% | 72.1% (191/265) | 45.7% (122/267) |
| diffusion | medium | 68.3% | 77.0% (181/235) | -- |
| **GAN** | low | **70.0%** | **72.9% (151/207)** | 45.6% (125/274) |
| **GAN** | medium | 62.5% | **79.1% (144/182)** | -- |

**Despite the alarming-looking loss curve, the WGAN-GP is a genuine contender -- narrowly the best of the three on correct-direction at both fidelities, and best-or-tied on low-fidelity validity.** It's also the least fidelity-stable of the three (validity 70.0% -> 62.5%, a 7.5-point drop vs. diffusion's 0.9-point drop and cVAE's 12.5-point drop) -- squarely between the other two on that axis, not a clean win-everywhere story. Signal above its own chance-level control: **72.9% - 45.6% = 27.3 points**, essentially tied with diffusion's 26.4 and both roughly double the cVAE's 13.2.

**The chance-baseline asymmetry noted in the earlier version of this section is now a clearer pattern, not just a curiosity.** All three null baselines sit off 50%, but they split cleanly by architecture *shape*, not just by chance: the two pure-noise-conditioned generators (diffusion 45.7%, GAN 45.6% -- agreeing with each other to within 0.1 point) sit *below* 50%, while the encoder-decoder cVAE sits *above* it (54.7%). Still not explained mechanistically, but two independent architectures landing within 0.1 point of each other on a "chance" number that isn't actually 50% is a real signal, not noise -- something about having an encoder shaping the latent (vs. sampling directly from a fixed prior) plausibly biases how the `baseline_e` mean-of-validity-candidates estimator sits relative to individual resamples, in a consistent direction. Worth understanding, not just noting, before the next architecture gets added to this comparison.

> **Retracted in §19.** A rerun of these exact null configs (same anchors/seed/model/fidelity) found cVAE at 46.1% and diffusion at 51.9% -- both **crossed sides of 50%** from the numbers above. Root cause: `--seed` never seeded torch's RNG, only numpy's (anchor selection) -- every "identical" rerun was silently decoding different candidates. Fixed in `eval_cvae_steerability.py`. The "agrees to 0.1 point, real pattern" framing above was premature; read this paragraph's numbers as one noisy draw, not a finding.

**Open:**
- The chance-baseline pattern (VAE above 50%, pure generators below, agreeing to 0.1 point) is now a real pattern worth a mechanistic explanation, not just a flagged asymmetry -- higher priority than before now that a third data point confirmed it's structured, not random.
- Single seed for all three models, both fidelities -- same standing caveat as §17.
- Neither diffusion's `T=200`/schedule nor the GAN's `n_critic`/`lr`/architecture were tuned at all, just matched to reasonable defaults and to the cVAE's eval-time cost where applicable -- real headroom on both probably left on the table, especially for the GAN given how unsettled its training loss looked.
- GAN sample diversity/mode collapse was never checked directly (only convergence + direction, via the oracle) -- a generator that learned to produce a narrow set of "safe" outputs per conditioning could still score well on this test without being a genuinely healthy GAN; worth a direct diversity check (e.g. pairwise distance among a target spot's own k candidates) before trusting it as much as the numbers alone suggest.
- The cluster-wise (held-out target-region) test is still the real remaining question for *any* of the three models -- this section answers "which generative architecture is strongest," not "does any of them generalize beyond what's already in the dataset," which was always the actual point of building a generative model here (§9).

## 19. Step-magnitude sweep and a selectivity metric: two more nuances than a single-step correct-direction rate captures

Direct follow-up to a sharp observation: a single fixed `--step-std=1.0` conflates two different questions the correct-direction rate alone can't separate -- (1) as the requested step gets larger, do candidates stay valid at all, and (2) when nudging one target, do the *other* 10 stay put, or does the whole vector drift together? Neither was previously measured; `--step-std` was a single arbitrary value and nothing tracked off-target movement.

**Built a selectivity metric into the existing harness** (no new script needed): every converged steer candidate's full 11-dim measured vector, not just the requested dimension, is now compared against `baseline_e`, z-scored by each target's own `t_std` so all 11 targets are in comparable units (raw eval-space units aren't -- a `qi` delta of 0.02 and an `aspect_ratio` delta of 0.02 mean very different things). Reports median on-target `|z-delta|` vs. median off-target mean `|z-delta|` (averaged over the other 10 dims) per direction and overall; their ratio is "selectivity" -- 1 means the requested target moved no differently than an unrequested one (no selectivity at all), higher means real isolation.

**Swept `--step-std` over {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} for the GAN (current best-of-three), same 12 anchors/seed, low fidelity throughout.** Note the "validity hit rate" header stat is measured at the anchor's own *unperturbed* target and is independent of `--step-std` by construction -- the number that actually answers "does validity degrade with magnitude" is the steer candidates' own convergence rate, averaged across the 11 per-target rows:

| step_std | steer-candidate convergence (mean across 11 targets) | selectivity ratio | median on-target \|z\| | median off-target \|z\| |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0.5 | **67.4%** | 1.06 | 0.27 | 0.26 |
| 1.0 | **53.8%** | 1.31 | 0.41 | 0.31 |
| 2.0 | **40.7%** | 2.13 | 0.99 | 0.46 |

**Two clean, opposite-direction trends, both real:**

1. **Convergence degrades steadily with requested magnitude** -- 67.4% -> 53.8% -> 40.7%, no cliff, a smooth cost curve. Matches physical intuition (bigger asks push further from the training distribution's density) and gives a first empirical answer to "how far can you push in target space and still mostly get something real": at this model/fidelity, roughly half your candidates are still valid at a 1-std ask, but you're down to ~40% by 2 std.

2. **Selectivity improves with requested magnitude** -- 1.06 (no better than chance) -> 1.31 -> 2.13. At a small step, the model isn't distinguishing "move this one target a little" from "move everything a little" at all -- on-target and off-target movement are statistically the same size. Only at a larger, more confident ask does the model's response actually concentrate on the requested dimension (on-target movement ~2x the off-target average by step=2.0).

**Reading: there's a real tradeoff, not a simple "smaller steps are safer" story.** Small, cautious requests are more likely to produce *something* valid, but that something is nearly as likely to have moved in an unrequested dimension as the requested one -- the model doesn't clearly know what you're asking for at small magnitudes. Larger requests are riskier (much lower hit rate) but the survivors are more reliably going where they were asked to go. Neither regime is strictly "better" -- which one matters depends on whether the downstream use case can tolerate a lot of rejected candidates in exchange for cleaner direction, or needs a high hit rate and can tolerate noisier targeting.

**Extended to all three models via a proper unattended ablation job.** Per explicit request to make the machinery reusable beyond this one VMEC++ task ("anything of the form where we have some parameters that get scored into something"), the domain-specific pieces were split out before scaling this up:

- `oracle_base.py` -- documents the interface a domain must implement (params -> named scores or failure).
- `vmec_oracle.py` -- this project's actual implementation (the worker subprocess, `FIDELITY_PRESETS`, `ZERO_INDICES`, `TARGET_NAMES`), the only file that knows anything about VMEC++ or stellarator boundaries.
- `oracle_harness.py` -- the domain-agnostic subprocess-per-candidate + hard-timeout batch runner (`run_batch_with_timeout(candidates, worker_fn, n_workers, timeout_s)`), parametrized by whatever `worker_fn` the oracle provides instead of hardcoding VMEC++.
- `eval_cvae_steerability.py` -- refactored to depend only on the abstract interface; `main()` split into a callable `run_eval(args)` so an orchestrator can drive many configs in-process (no per-config Docker/Python startup cost, no shelling out).
- `run_ablation.py` -- new orchestrator: 3 models x {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} step_std at low fidelity + one medium-fidelity confirmation + one null control per model = 18 configs, resumable (skips configs whose output already exists), writes a running consolidated summary after every config rather than only at the end.

Verified the refactor before trusting it with an unattended multi-hour run: reran the GAN's already-known step_std=1.0/low config through the new code path and confirmed matching numbers before launching the full grid.

**Full grid, same 12 anchors/seed=0, all three models (steer-candidate convergence, not the step-independent anchor-spot validity stat):**

| model | step_std | steer-candidate convergence | correct-direction | selectivity |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| cVAE | 0.5 | 66.7% | 56.8% | 0.88 |
| cVAE | 1.0 | 61.4% | 62.1% | 1.02 |
| cVAE | 1.5 | 61.4% | 74.1% | 1.35 |
| cVAE | 2.0 | 53.5% | 75.5% | 1.31 |
| diffusion | 0.5 | 70.2% | 67.2% | 0.98 |
| diffusion | 1.0 | 70.7% | 69.3% | 1.38 |
| diffusion | 1.5 | 66.2% | 71.9% | 1.19 |
| diffusion | 2.0 | 57.3% | 81.1% | 1.76 |
| GAN | 0.5 | 65.7% | 75.1% | 1.09 |
| GAN | 1.0 | 55.1% | 75.5% | 1.45 |
| GAN | 1.5 | 46.5% | 74.5% | 1.64 |
| GAN | 2.0 | 39.9% | 76.7% | 1.85 |

**The GAN-only pattern replicates directionally in all three architectures -- selectivity rises with step magnitude, every time -- but the *shape* of the convergence/precision tradeoff is not universal:**

- **GAN degrades fastest and cleanest with magnitude** (66% -> 40%, a steep, near-linear decline matching the earlier 3-point sweep almost exactly -- a real, reproducible property of this architecture, not a fluke of the first sweep). Its correct-direction rate is nearly flat across all four steps (75-77%) -- it's already about as directionally accurate at a cautious 0.5-std ask as at 2.0 std, unlike the other two.
- **cVAE and diffusion degrade much more gently** (cVAE 67%->54%, diffusion 70%->57%) but both show correct-direction rate climbing substantially with magnitude (cVAE 57%->76%, diffusion 67%->81%) -- these two architectures need a bigger, riskier ask before they reliably show correct direction at all, where the GAN already "knows" the direction even for small asks.
- **Selectivity climbs with magnitude for every model**, but from different starting points and to different ceilings: cVAE 0.88->1.31, diffusion 0.98->1.76 (the single largest selectivity gain of the three), GAN 1.09->1.85 (highest absolute ceiling). All three start near 1.0 (no selectivity) at step=0.5 and end clearly above it.

**A real mistake caught in the process of running this: §18's "opposite-sign chance-baseline pattern, two architectures agreeing to within 0.1 point" does not replicate and should be retracted.** This ablation's null-mode reruns (nominally identical to §18's: same 12 anchors, same seed, same model, same fidelity) gave meaningfully different numbers:

| model | §18 null correct-direction | this run's null correct-direction |
|---|--:|--:|
| cVAE | 54.7% | **46.1%** |
| diffusion | 45.7% | **51.9%** |
| GAN | 45.6% | 46.6% |

cVAE and diffusion's null baselines each swung 6-9 points and **crossed sides of 50%** between "identical" runs -- directly contradicting §18's claim that the VAE sits reliably above 50% and the two pure-generators reliably below. Root cause found and fixed: `--seed` only ever seeded `numpy` (anchor selection); every `torch.randn(...)` call in the actual stochastic decoding was unseeded, so nominally-identical reruns silently decoded entirely different candidates every time. Fixed with `torch.manual_seed(args.seed)` in `run_eval` and verified directly (same config run twice now produces byte-identical validity/selectivity numbers). **§18's null-baseline "pattern" should be read as noise, not a structural finding** -- the swing size between two "identical" runs (6-9 points) is comparable to or larger than the cross-architecture gap that was called a pattern. GAN's null happened to be the one that looked stable (45.6% -> 46.6%, only 1 point) -- likely why the pattern looked convincing with only one confirmatory rerun instead of several.

**Open:**
- Only 12 anchors / one seed (now genuinely one full seed, post-fix) per config -- the convergence/precision/selectivity trends above are each a single draw, not confirmed against a second independent seed. Given how badly the null-baseline claim just failed to replicate, none of the numbers in this section's table should be treated as more solid than "first look" until a second seed is run with the now-fixed reproducibility.
- Selectivity was only measured axis-aligned (nudge exactly one target, holding the other 10's *requested* value fixed) -- an arbitrary-direction move in target space is still untested.
- No chance-level control for selectivity specifically yet -- worth adding now that the correct-direction null control has already shown how easy it is to overtrust a single-run "chance" number.
- The cluster-wise (held-out target-region) test is still the deferred, harder question underneath all of this -- everything in §17-19 is about capability where the dataset already has coverage.

Direct follow-up to §19's own open list. Two of the three items resolved immediately from data already on disk or a quick reanalysis; the third needed new capability.

## 21. A second oracle domain: airfoils via XFOIL, and a real test of the genericization

§18-19's genericization work (`oracle_base.py`, `vmec_oracle.py`, `oracle_harness.py`) was built on a single example -- worth confirming it actually generalizes, not just that it looked generic in isolation. Investigated three open-source candidates with the "parameterization -> scored performance, or failure" shape and a route to bulk data generation:

- **TORAX** (Google DeepMind's JAX tokamak transport simulator, Apache 2.0, confirmed via the repo's own LICENSE file) -- physically complementary to VMEC++ (transport dynamics vs. equilibrium shape), and JAX-native means genuine GPU batching is possible rather than the subprocess-per-candidate pattern VMEC++ needs. Real unknowns going in: no confirmed runtime numbers, no confirmed failure-signaling behavior, and no existing public dataset of runs to bootstrap from -- flagged as needing a short empirical spike before a real build, not built this pass.
- **Airfoils via XFOIL** (Mark Drela's panel-method solver, `daniel-de-vries/xfoil-python`, GPL-3.0) as the fast primary oracle, OpenFOAM as a natural slow/confirmatory second tier -- the same low/fast-vs-medium/slow relationship VMEC++ already has. An existing bootstrap dataset was found too: [AirfRANS](https://github.com/Extrality/airfrans_lib) (1,000 OpenFOAM RANS airfoil simulations, NeurIPS 2022, ODC-ODbL license) with its generation pipeline (`Extrality/NACA_simulation`) also open, meaning more data beyond the fixed 1,000 is possible later. Chosen as the one to actually build this pass -- closest structural match to VMEC++ of anything found (existing dataset, fast primary oracle, real convergence-or-failure signal, natural multi-fidelity story).

**Built `scripts/airfoil_oracle.py`** implementing `oracle_base.py`'s interface: CST (Class-Shape Transformation, Kulfan 2008) parameterization, `PARAM_DIM=16` (8 upper + 8 lower Bernstein coefficients) -- chosen over the much lower-dimensional NACA 4/5-digit family specifically to be closer in spirit to VMEC++'s 90-coefficient expressiveness. Targets: `cl`, `cd`, `cm` straight from XFOIL plus a derived `l_over_d`, with `cd` flagged in `LOG_TARGET_NAMES` (same wide-dynamic-range reasoning as VMEC++'s `qi`/`max_elongation`). Aux conditioning (Reynolds, Mach, angle of attack) mirrors `n_field_periods`'s role for VMEC++.

**Two real installation problems hit and fixed, both the hard way, same as this project's own established pattern for this kind of thing:** `pip install xfoil` fails outright -- the PyPI sdist ships without the git submodule containing the actual XFOIL Fortran source (`CMakeLists.txt` missing at build time). Fixed by cloning from GitHub with `--recurse-submodules` and installing from the local checkout instead, which also needed `git` added to the image (not previously required by anything else in `Dockerfile.train`).

**Validated the oracle against a known-good shape before trusting it, same discipline as §8's VMEC++ validation:** a roughly NACA0012-like symmetric CST shape (constant coefficients, ~13% max thickness) at Re=1e6, alpha=5 deg gave `cl=0.504` (thin-airfoil theory predicts ~0.548 at this alpha; viscous effects account for the gap), `cd=0.0081`, `l_over_d=62` -- all physically sane. **Confirmed the convergence signal directly by reading the wrapper's own source, not assuming it**: `xf.a(alpha)` returns `(nan, nan, nan, nan)` whenever the underlying Fortran `conv` flag is false -- the same unambiguous converged-or-didn't contract VMEC++ provides. Mid-solve "MRCHDU: Convergence failed" boundary-layer messages can appear without the overall flag ever going false (confirmed on a NACA0012 sweep to alpha=40 that still returned finite values despite them) -- only the final `conv` flag is authoritative.

**Failure-mode and speed check through the real timeout harness**: 5 wild/random CST coefficient vectors (uniform in [-1.5, 1.5], well outside sane airfoil range) all correctly came back `False, "xfoil: did not converge"` -- no crashes, no hangs -- alongside the known-good shape converging normally. **All 6 candidates finished in 0.1s total** -- meaningfully faster than even VMEC++'s low-fidelity tier (~5-15s/call), consistent with the investigation's expectation that XFOIL would be the faster oracle of the two.

**Open:**
- Only stress-tested on 5 adversarial candidates -- VMEC++'s hang problem (§9) only showed up at real scale (2,592 attempts); XFOIL hasn't been pushed anywhere near that hard yet, so "no hangs seen" isn't the same claim as VMEC++'s eventually-confirmed "can hang" finding.
- No training data yet -- nothing analogous to `X.npy`/`Y.npy` exists for this domain. AirfRANS's 1,000 samples use the NACA family, not CST, so bootstrapping from it needs a NACA-to-CST refit step, not a direct load.
- No generative model, no eval harness run against this oracle yet -- `airfoil_oracle.py` is validated as an oracle in isolation, not yet plugged into `eval_cvae_steerability.py`'s `--oracle` selection (which doesn't actually exist as a CLI flag yet -- the harness currently imports `vmec_oracle` directly rather than dispatching on a name, a gap to close before this is a true drop-in second domain).
- OpenFOAM tier not built -- `FIDELITY_PRESETS` has only `"low"`.
- TORAX's empirical spike (runtime, failure signaling) is still undone.

**AirfRANS dataset size, asked about directly:** 1,000 simulations (tiny next to ConStellaration's 158,685 rows), but unlike ConStellaration's compact scalar-only format, each AirfRANS sample is a **full 2D CFD field solution** (pressure/velocity over a several-thousand-node mesh), not just integrated force coefficients -- multi-GB on disk for the full set (exact figure not pinned down; a HuggingFace parquet mirror showed 3.2GB for a partial ~900-row slice, consistent with "multi-GB for all 1,000," not "crazy" but not ConStellaration-small either). Doesn't actually matter much for this project's purposes, though: we only need the integrated `cl`/`cd`/`cm` + geometry per row, not the full field -- a distilled version would be closer to ConStellaration's own footprint. Also AirfRANS uses the NACA 4/5-digit family, not CST, so bootstrapping from it needs a refit step regardless of raw size (already an open item above).

**NeuralFoil (Sharpe, MIT) investigated per direct user suggestion -- benchmarked against our real XFOIL oracle rather than adopted on the strength of its own README.** MIT license, `pip install neuralfoil`, pure NumPy/CasADi (no Fortran compile step, unlike XFOIL). Pulls in `aerosandbox`, which upgrades numpy to 2.x -- same conflict as vmecpp/constellaration's own numpy issue (§8), fixed the same way (re-pin `numpy<2` immediately after install, verified both `torch.from_numpy` and `neuralfoil` still work). Its `get_aero_from_kulfan_parameters` API takes `upper_weights`/`lower_weights` (8 each) directly -- the exact CST parameterization `airfoil_oracle.py` already uses, no adapter needed beyond zeroing `leading_edge_weight`/`TE_thickness` (which `airfoil_oracle.py` doesn't model either) and transposing to `(8, n_batch)` (confirmed by trial -- the batching axis convention isn't `(n_batch, 8)` as the docstring's "leading dimension" phrasing suggests, submitting that shape raises a clear `ValueError`).

**Explicit stance, matching this project's standing discipline (§6/§8 onward): not a replacement oracle.** NeuralFoil is a fitted emulator of XFOIL, not physics -- exactly the kind of thing this project has repeatedly found reason not to trust blindly. Benchmarked instead as an auxiliary signal: 40 shapes generated as graded perturbations of the same known-good CST shape (noise std 0.02/0.1/0.3/1.2, 10 each), scored by both real XFOIL (through the actual timeout harness) and NeuralFoil (one batched call).

| noise | XFOIL convergence | mean confidence (XFOIL converged) | mean confidence (XFOIL failed) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 0.02 | 90% | 0.952 | 0.956 |
| 0.10 | 80% | 0.893 | 0.170 |
| 0.30 | 40% | 0.054 | 0.002 |
| 1.20 | 0% | n/a | 0.000 |

**Reading:** NeuralFoil's confidence flag cleanly separates genuinely bad shapes (noise=1.2: confidence 0 across the board, matching XFOIL's 0% convergence exactly) from mildly-perturbed ones (noise=0.1: 0.893 vs. 0.170, a huge gap that tracks XFOIL's own convergence split well) -- but it is **not** a clean proxy for "will XFOIL converge" in between: at noise=0.02, confidence sits ~0.95 whether XFOIL converged or not (too coarse to catch the rare failure this close to a good shape); at noise=0.3, confidence is low even on the 40% that *did* converge in XFOIL (conservative -- flags borderline-but-real cases as unreliable too). Where confidence is high, `cl`/`cd` agreement with real XFOIL is good (mostly within ~0.01-0.04 for `cl`, matching NeuralFoil's own stated error margins) but not exact -- a few noise=0.1 shapes disagreed with XFOIL by ~0.04 in `cl` despite confidence >0.85.

**Speed, measured on the same 40-shape batch:** XFOIL through our subprocess-per-candidate harness, 1.77s total (44ms/shape, mixed convergence/failure). NeuralFoil, one batched call, no subprocess needed at all: 14.5ms total for all 40 -- **0.36ms/shape, roughly 120x faster**, consistent with the project's own README-stated claims but now confirmed directly rather than assumed.

**Verdict:** worth integrating as a fast pre-filter / secondary signal ahead of real XFOIL calls during bulk data generation -- confirmed reliable at cleanly rejecting genuine garbage (which is most of what a from-scratch generative model would produce early on, the same role structural validation plays for VMEC++ boundaries at ~0.02s), not reliable enough as a stand-in for XFOIL itself given the confidence-vs-actual-convergence mismatch in the moderate-perturbation regime. Not built yet: an actual pre-filter integration into the candidate-generation loop (nothing currently calls NeuralFoil before XFOIL).

## 22. First airfoil baseline: dataset, cVAE, validity + steerability + selectivity

Direct follow-up to §21 -- "do we have baselines" for the airfoil domain, mirroring the VMEC++ pipeline (§16-19) as closely as the domain allows. Confirms the genericized harness (oracle_base.py/oracle_harness.py) actually pays off: this took one new dataset-generation script and one training script, not a rebuild of the eval machinery.

**Built `scripts/generate_airfoil_dataset.py`** -- no existing CST-parameterized dataset to bootstrap from (§21), so this generates one from scratch: a handful of archetypal CST seeds (thin/thick, symmetric/cambered) perturbed by Gaussian noise at a randomized scale, Reynolds sampled log-uniform over [1e5, 1e7], angle of attack uniform over [-5, 15] (matching AirfRANS's own range). **50,011 accepted rows in 3,201s (~53 min) at a steady 56.4% hit rate** -- run concurrently with the VMEC++ seed-1 ablation on the same machine, some slowdown from sharing cores but nothing broke.

**Caught a real data-quality gap before training on it, not after:** `l_over_d` (`cl/cd`, computed in `airfoil_oracle.py`) had absurd outliers up to 9.8e10 -- a small number of candidates "converge" (XFOIL's `conv` flag true) with a spuriously near-zero `cd` (down to ~1e-12), not real 2D-airfoil drag at any Reynolds number in this project's range. Confirmed narrow before filtering rather than assumed: 127/50,011 rows (0.25%). Fixed at the source (`airfoil_oracle.py`'s `worker_fn` now rejects `cd < 1e-6` as non-physical, not just non-converged) so future runs don't reproduce it, plus a training-time sanity filter in `train_airfoil_cvae.py` for this already-generated dataset. Same "the convergence flag alone doesn't guarantee physical sanity at the tails" lesson VMEC++'s own reproduction-gap investigation (§17) already taught, now confirmed a second time in a second domain.

**Built `scripts/train_airfoil_cvae.py` and `scripts/eval_airfoil_steerability.py`, reusing `train_cvae.py`'s `CVAE` class unchanged** -- confirmed its `n_nfp` constructor argument is genuinely just "extra conditioning dim count," not VMEC-specific despite the name: passed `n_nfp=2` for airfoils' two *continuous* aux values (log-Reynolds, angle of attack) concatenated directly, in place of VMEC's discrete one-hot. No changes needed to the model class itself. Same baseline discipline as VMEC++ (mean of the anchor's own converged validity candidates, never a re-verification of a stored value) even though the specific reproduction-gap mechanism that motivated it doesn't apply here (this dataset was never touched by a foreign pipeline).

| target | targeted correct-direction | null (chance) | signal above chance | targeted selectivity | null selectivity |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| cl | 90.0% | 33.3% | **+56.7pp** | 0.43 | 0.43 |
| cd | 55.2% | 46.9% | +8.3pp | 0.84 | 0.82 |
| cm | 96.2% | 50.0% | **+46.2pp** | 3.20 | 2.47 |
| l_over_d | 50.0% | 44.8% | +5.2pp | 0.56 | 0.63 |
| **aggregate** | **68.8%** | **43.8%** | **+25.0pp** | -- | -- |

Validity hit rate at the anchor's own spot: **85.8%** (103/120) -- notably higher than VMEC++'s cVAE (55.8% at the same step_std, §18), consistent with airfoils/XFOIL being the more forgiving domain throughout this investigation (§21's speed/robustness findings).

But it's very uneven by target: `cl` and `cm` show strong, real steering (+56.7pp, +46.2pp) while `cd` and `l_over_d` are barely above chance (+8.3pp, +5.2pp) -- worth noting `l_over_d` is a *derived* quantity (cl/cd computed post-hoc, not something XFOIL reports independently), so asking to move it directly conflates two underlying physical quantities in a way the model may not have learned to disentangle; `cd`'s own weakness is unexplained. **Selectivity is essentially unchanged between targeted and null for every target** (e.g. cl: 0.43 vs. 0.43, cd: 0.84 vs. 0.82) -- unlike correct-direction, selectivity here doesn't appear to be a response to the steering request at all; it reads more like a fixed property of how tightly each target is coupled to the others in this model's learned manifold than something steering *causes*. Different from what VMEC++'s selectivity metric showed (§19-20: rose with request magnitude) -- worth understanding before trusting selectivity as a meaningful signal in this domain, since it may simply need a different baseline discipline than the axis-aligned magnitude sweep gave it meaning through for VMEC++.

**Open:**
- Single seed, single step_std, one architecture (cVAE only) -- none of diffusion/GAN, no magnitude sweep, no second seed built for this domain yet, all real gaps §17-20's VMEC++ work closed before being trusted.
- `cd`/`l_over_d`'s weak signal is a real, unexplained finding, not yet investigated (architecture limitation? these two targets' own dynamic range/log-transform interacting badly with the conditioning? worth a direct comparison against VMEC++'s own per-target spread before concluding either way).
- Selectivity's null-invariance is itself an open, unexplained finding -- possibly means the selectivity metric needs the same "vary the step size and watch it move" grounding §19 gave it for VMEC++ before it's trustworthy here.
- No diffusion/GAN airfoil baselines yet, so no cross-architecture comparison the way §18 gave VMEC++.
- The cd<1e-6 physical-sanity floor is a single hand-picked threshold, not derived from anything principled -- fine for a first pass, worth revisiting if it turns out to be cutting into real (if extreme) L/D territory.

## 23. Airfoil diffusion + GAN: closing §22's "cVAE only" gap

Direct follow-up to §22's own open list -- same architectures, same conditioning trick already used for the cVAE (§22: `n_nfp=2` for continuous log-Reynolds + angle-of-attack aux, in place of VMEC's discrete one-hot), reused unchanged again. `scripts/train_airfoil_diffusion.py` and `scripts/train_airfoil_gan.py` are near-verbatim copies of `train_airfoil_cvae.py`'s data loading (same physical-sanity filter, same normalization discipline) wired to `train_diffusion.py`'s `DiffusionDenoiser`/`train_gan.py`'s `Generator`+`Critic` instead of `train_cvae.py`'s `CVAE` -- no changes needed to any of the three model classes themselves, confirming the same genericization result §22 found for the cVAE extends to the other two architectures. `eval_airfoil_steerability.py` gained the same `--model-type {cvae,diffusion,gan}` dispatch `eval_cvae_steerability.py` already had, via a `load_generative_model()` factored out the same way.

- `cd`/`l_over_d`'s weak signal (§22) wasn't re-examined per-architecture here -- worth checking whether diffusion/GAN show the same lopsided per-target pattern (`cl`/`cm` strong, `cd`/`l_over_d` weak) or whether that's cVAE-specific too.

## 24. Auditing the cVAE/diffusion/GAN comparison itself: param/compute parity and two correctness checks

Direct response to a user question, after §18-23 repeatedly found diffusion and GAN beating the cVAE across two domains: is that a real architectural finding, or a methodology artifact (unmatched capacity/compute, or a bug specific to how one architecture is sampled)? Built `scripts/audit_baselines.py` to check empirically against the real checkpoints rather than reason about it -- four checks, run against both domains.

**[1] Param count at generation time (what actually runs to produce one candidate, not total params -- the cVAE's encoder is train-only and correctly excluded): cVAE decoder and GAN generator are *exactly* matched** (101,466/101,466 params VMEC++, 79,888/79,888 airfoils -- not approximately, identical to the digit, as §18 already noted for VMEC++ and now confirmed for airfoils too). Diffusion's single denoiser network is ~2.04-2.08x larger (206,810 / 166,288) -- bigger, not matched, though not wildly so; it also has to process a time embedding the other two don't need.

**[2] Training compute is NOT matched, by design of WGAN-GP, not by accident:** "200 epochs" sounds like the same training budget across all three, but isn't. cVAE and diffusion each get one gradient-update step per network per batch (61,800 over 200 epochs on VMEC++, 19,400 on airfoils). The GAN's critic gets `n_critic=5` update steps per generator step -- 309,000 critic updates on VMEC++, 5x the other two networks' own step count. This is standard WGAN-GP practice (the critic needs to stay well-trained relative to the generator for the gradient penalty to mean anything), not a bug to fix, but it does mean the GAN was given more total training compute than "200 epochs" implies at face value -- worth stating plainly next to every GAN-wins result rather than leaving "same epochs" to imply "same compute."

**[3] Inference compute is drastically NOT matched, and this is the sharpest finding:** every eval script in this project generates one candidate via a single forward pass for the cVAE and GAN, but via a full T=200-step sequential ancestral sampling chain for diffusion. **Diffusion gets 200x the inference-time compute of the other two architectures, for every single candidate scored in every eval in §18-23.** `train_diffusion.py`'s own docstring already flags this (T was picked for wall-clock parity with the eval harness's time budget, not FLOP parity) but the audit puts a number on it: diffusion's validity/steerability edge over the cVAE could be partly "200x more compute per sample," not purely a better-understood target manifold. This doesn't invalidate the diffusion-vs-cVAE ranking (GAN, which is NOT compute-favored at inference -- also 1 forward pass, matched with the cVAE -- beats the cVAE by an even larger margin in most of §18-23's numbers), but it does mean **diffusion's specific numbers, wherever they lead the pack, should be read with this caveat attached.**

**[4a] cVAE prior-matching -- real bug in the *audit itself* caught before trusting the first result, not a bug in the model.** Checked whether the encoder's aggregate posterior over the real dataset actually resembles the N(0, I) prior that `build_candidates` samples `z` from at generation time (with `--beta=0.01`, the KL term is only lightly enforced, so this isn't guaranteed). First pass on the airfoil checkpoint came back looking catastrophic -- aggregate variance per latent dim averaging 383,392 (should be ~1), some dims blown up past 2.8 million. Before writing that up as a finding, checked the mechanism directly (same discipline as §17's reproduction-gap investigation): the audit script had fed the *unfiltered* `airfoil_Y.npy` through z-scoring stats computed on the *filtered* training set, so the 127/50,011 known-bad rows (§22's `cd`-near-zero/`l_over_d`-blowup outliers) produced enormous out-of-distribution conditioning vectors into the encoder. Applying the same physical-sanity filter `train_airfoil_cvae.py` trains on fixed it: **real aggregate variance per latent dim is 0.99 (airfoils) / 0.877 (VMEC++)** -- both close to the ideal 1.0, not catastrophic. There is a real, milder, well-known VAE phenomenon underneath, checked per-dimension: roughly half the airfoil cVAE's 32 latent dims are near-fully posterior-collapsed (encoder mu barely varies across the dataset, within-sample std ≈1 -- the decoder has learned to ignore them) while the other half are used almost deterministically (mu spans ~unit variance, within-sample std as low as 0.04-0.14). Both halves individually still sum to ≈1 total variance, so the *aggregate* z ~ N(0, I) sampling distribution isn't mismatched -- but only about half the latent capacity is actually doing generative work. Not a correctness bug, a `--beta=0.01`-driven capacity-underuse pattern common to lightly-regularized VAEs; worth knowing, not urgent to fix.

**[4b] Diffusion's noise schedule has a real, unfixed correctness gap.** `make_schedule`'s linear beta(1e-4 -> 0.02) is the schedule tuned for the DDPM paper's T=1000; reused unchanged at this project's T=200 (chosen for eval-harness wall-clock, per [3] above). Computed alpha_bar at t=T-1 directly: **0.132** (sqrt(alpha_bar)=0.364 residual signal, sqrt(1-alpha_bar)=0.932 noise) -- at T=1000 this quantity is ~1e-5, i.e. genuinely fully destroyed. At T=200 it is not: training's forward diffusion process never actually produces an x_{T-1} that's pure noise, but `ddpm_sample`'s ancestral sampling *starts* generation from `x_T ~ N(0, I)`, pure noise by construction. That's a real train/sample distribution mismatch, present in both `train_diffusion.py` and `train_airfoil_diffusion.py` (same schedule, same T, both domains). Notably, this bug -- if it has a directional effect at all -- would work *against* diffusion's reported advantage, not inflate it, since the model is being asked at generation time to denoise from a starting distribution it never actually trained against; diffusion still won or tied on every metric in §18-23 despite this. Flagged as worth fixing (a shorter, T=200-tuned schedule, or a cosine schedule, would close the gap) but not yet fixed -- would mean retraining both diffusion checkpoints and rerunning every affected eval, not done without explicit go-ahead given how much of §18-23's diffusion numbers that touches.

**Bottom line:** no bug found that would explain away diffusion or GAN's advantage over the cVAE -- the GAN's generator is exactly param-matched to the cVAE's decoder and still wins, on both domains, on every metric. The two real gaps found both cut in directions that don't favor the existing "diffusion/GAN beat cVAE" story: the GAN's edge could be partly attributed to more training compute (by design, not incidentally), and diffusion's edge carries a 200x inference-compute asymmetry that should accompany the number whenever it's cited, plus an unresolved schedule bug that (if anything) has been working against diffusion's own reported numbers, not for them.

**Open:**
- No compute-matched *rerun* was attempted (e.g. cVAE/GAN given 200x more inference-time forward passes some other way, or diffusion capped to 1); [3]'s finding is a real asymmetry surfaced, not yet neutralized.
- `--beta` isn't recorded in any of the six checkpoints' saved state -- confirmed by reading `train_cvae.py`/`train_airfoil_cvae.py`'s `torch.save(...)` dict directly, every training run so far used the CLI default (0.01), but nothing on disk would catch a future run that didn't.

## 25. TORAX evaluated for real: pip-installable, cleanly licensed, genuinely excellent failure signaling -- but doesn't fit this project's harness without real new infrastructure

Direct follow-up to §21's own open item ("TORAX's empirical spike -- runtime, failure signaling -- is still undone") and a direct user request to evaluate https://github.com/google-deepmind/torax for real, not from the README's own claims. Same discipline as every other oracle candidate in this project: installed it, ran it, read its source for the failure contract, rather than trusting documentation.

**License, confirmed by fetching the actual LICENSE file:** Apache 2.0 for the core package; MIT for a small set of PINT geometry data files under `torax/data/third_party/geo/`; CC BY 4.0 for STEP-project geometry files. All permissive, no blocker.

**Install: genuinely clean, unlike XFOIL's git-submodule problem.** `pip install torax` resolves and installs a pure-wheel dependency tree with no compiler/build-tool step at all (confirmed in a real fresh `python:3.12-slim` container) -- torax 1.4.3, ~60 transitive packages, all as prebuilt wheels. **But it hard-requires Python >=3.12 and numpy>=2** (confirmed directly in `pyproject.toml` and by the real install pulling numpy 2.5.2) -- this project's existing `train` image is Python 3.10.13 and its `oracle` image is Python 3.11.14, and *both* pin `numpy<2` specifically for reasons already hit twice before (vmecpp/constellaration and neuralfoil/aerosandbox both silently want numpy>=2, breaking `torch.from_numpy` against this image's torch 2.2.0 build -- §8, §21). TORAX cannot be added to either existing image with a `RUN pip install` line the way XFOIL and NeuralFoil were; it needs a genuinely separate image built on a newer Python base, a bigger lift than either prior oracle domain needed.

**Ran the bundled `basic_config` example end-to-end for real timing** (`run_torax --config=<path> --output_dir=...`, CPU-only container): completed successfully, wrote a real NetCDF output file. TORAX's own reported breakdown: "Sim and params build time: 0.04s, simulation time: 5.65s" for 100 timesteps covering 5 simulated seconds of plasma physics -- but that 5.65s is dominated by a one-time JIT-compilation cost the run log itself flags: "The first step took more than 2.0 std devs longer than other steps... 3.22s of wall clock time." After that one-time compile, the actual physics stepping is fast: "Simulated 5.00s of physics in 0.23s of wall clock time" (~2.3ms/step for the remaining 99 steps).

**This surfaces a real, previously-unknown architectural mismatch with this project's existing oracle harness.** `oracle_harness.py`'s `run_batch_with_timeout` -- the shared harness both VMEC++ and airfoils/XFOIL use -- is a subprocess-per-candidate pattern: every candidate design gets a fresh process. VMEC++ and XFOIL are both fine with this because each call's actual physics-solve cost dominates any process-startup overhead. TORAX inverts that: **most of its cost is a one-time ~3.2s JIT-compile tax, not the physics itself**, and JAX does not persist compiled functions across fresh processes by default. A subprocess-per-candidate harness would pay that ~3.2s compile tax on *every single candidate*, making TORAX slower than VMEC++'s own low-fidelity preset for bulk generation -- the opposite of the "JAX-native means genuine batching" advantage §21 originally expected going in. Realizing TORAX's actual speed (sub-3ms/step after compilation) needs a long-lived worker process evaluating a stream of parameter sets in-process (ideally via `jax.vmap` across many candidates at once, which TORAX's JAX-native design should support but which hasn't been tested here), not this project's existing timeout-subprocess pattern -- a real harness redesign, not a drop-in.

**Failure signaling is the strongest finding, and it's better than either existing oracle's.** Read the source directly rather than assuming: `torax._src.state.SimError` is a proper 7-value enum (`NO_ERROR`, `NAN_DETECTED`, `QUASINEUTRALITY_BROKEN`, `NEGATIVE_CORE_PROFILES`, `REACHED_MIN_DT`, `LOW_TEMPERATURE_COLLAPSE`, `DID_NOT_REACH_T_FINAL`), each with its own descriptive error message pointing at a likely physical cause. It's a first-class return value from the actual simulation runner -- confirmed by reading `torax._src.orchestration.run_simulation`'s signature directly: `states_history, post_processed_outputs_history, sim_error = run_loop.run_loop(...)`. This maps directly onto `oracle_base.py`'s `worker_fn` contract (`ok = sim_error == SimError.NO_ERROR`) more cleanly than VMEC++'s or XFOIL's simple binary converged-or-didn't, since a failed TORAX run also says *which of 7 ways* it failed.

**No existing public bulk dataset**, same gap §21 already found -- would need building from scratch the way `generate_airfoil_dataset.py` did for airfoils, and the JIT-tax problem above means that build is a bigger lift than airfoils' was.

**Verdict: usable, not a quick add.** Real, permissively licensed, cleanly installable, with the best failure-signaling contract of any oracle domain looked at so far -- but two genuine integration costs, both now concrete rather than speculative: (1) a new Docker image on Python >=3.12 / numpy>=2, isolated from the existing numpy<2-pinned images; (2) a harness built around a long-lived batched JAX worker instead of `oracle_harness.py`'s subprocess-per-candidate pattern, to actually realize TORAX's speed rather than paying its JIT tax on every call. Not started -- would want a small spike specifically confirming `jax.vmap`-style batching or `JAX_COMPILATION_CACHE_DIR` actually avoids the recompile cost across distinct parameter draws before committing to the full build, the same way `airfoil_oracle.py` was validated against a known-good shape before anything was built on top of it.

**Open:**
- `jax.vmap`/persistent-compilation-cache mitigation for the JIT tax is proposed, not tested.
- No training dataset, no generative model, no eval harness integration -- purely an oracle-level feasibility check, same stage airfoils were at after §21 before §22 built on top of it.
- Only the bundled `basic_config` example was run: a genuinely adversarial/failure-triggering config (to confirm `SimError` fires in practice, not just that the enum exists in source) hasn't been tried, unlike airfoils' 5-adversarial-candidate check in §21.

**Can the ConStellaration stellarator data seed TORAX designs? No -- checked directly, not assumed.** Read `torax.examples.basic_config`'s actual config dict: TORAX geometry is either a simple scalar type (`geometry_type: 'circular'`, used for the bundled test/prototyping configs) or loaded from a real tokamak equilibrium file (CHEASE/geqdsk-format, describing a 2D axisymmetric poloidal cross-section via things like major/minor radius, elongation, triangularity). Stellarator boundaries in this project's `X.npy` are a fundamentally different kind of object: 90 Fourier coefficients over a genuinely 3D non-axisymmetric surface (toroidal mode structure, `n_field_periods` discrete symmetry) -- there's no natural mapping from one representation to the other, so the raw coefficient values aren't reusable as literal TORAX inputs. What *would* carry over, if this domain gets built: the general bootstrap methodology `generate_airfoil_dataset.py` used (perturb a handful of known-good archetypal seeds with graded noise, since raw-random params essentially never converge -- confirmed for both VMEC++ boundaries, §9, and CST airfoil shapes, §22's own generation run). TORAX ships several real example configs (`basic_config`, `iterhybrid_rampup`, `iterhybrid_predictor_corrector`, `step_flattop_bgb`) that could serve as exactly this kind of archetypal-seed set -- untested here, flagged as the natural next step if this domain gets built, not assumed to work without checking (perturbing a tokamak transport config isn't guaranteed to behave like perturbing a shape parameterization does).

## 26. Novelty and mode-collapse audit: are generated designs actually new, or is this retrieval wearing a generative-model costume?

Direct user question, and a real gap explicitly flagged as open back in §18 ("Not yet checked: GAN sample diversity/mode collapse... a generator producing a narrow 'safe' output set per spec could still score well here"). Built `scripts/audit_novelty_diversity.py` -- pure parameter-space geometry against real data, no oracle calls needed at all, so this ran in minutes against all 6 (domain x architecture) checkpoints.

**Method**: for 12 anchors (same random-seed anchor selection as every other eval in this project), decode 30 candidates per anchor and measure two things in the checkpoint's own standardized coefficient space: (1) each candidate's nearest-neighbor distance to the closest row in the *real* training set, compared against a reference -- the same nearest-neighbor distance computed among real rows themselves (leave-one-out) -- i.e. "how far apart are real designs from each other, typically"; (2) the mean pairwise distance among the 30 candidates decoded at the *same* anchor (same conditioning, different latent noise only) -- mode collapse would show up as this being near zero.

**Finding 1 -- no memorization, anywhere, by any architecture, in either domain.** Near-duplicate fraction (generated candidate landing within 1% of the real-to-real reference distance of some specific real row) is **0.0% for all 6 (domain, architecture) combinations**, over 360 generated candidates per combination. Nothing is copying training rows.

**Finding 2 -- generated designs sit at a real distance from the training data, and that distance differs meaningfully by domain.** Airfoils: generated-to-real distance is close to the real-to-real reference (ratio 1.06-1.17x across cVAE/diffusion/GAN) -- generated designs occupy new territory at roughly the same spacing real designs occupy relative to each other, a healthy reading. Stellarators: generated-to-real distance is **2.8-4.5x** the real-to-real reference (cVAE farthest at 4.48x, GAN closest to real data at 2.82x) -- VMEC++ models are straying considerably farther from the training manifold than airfoils' models do. This lines up with everything else already found about the two domains: VMEC++ validity rates (55.8-70.0%, §18) are much lower than airfoils' (85.8-91.7%, §23) -- designs that wander farther from where real, physically-valid examples are known to sit are exactly the designs more likely to fail the oracle. Not a new, independent finding so much as a second data source landing on the same conclusion airfoils' higher validity already implied.

**Finding 3 -- no mode collapse, including in the GAN, the architecture with the worst reputation for it.** Within-anchor diversity (30 different-latent-noise decodes at one fixed target) exceeds the real-to-real reference distance in every single case: 3.9-6.2x for VMEC++, 2.6-2.9x for airfoils. The GAN has the *lowest* diversity ratio of the three architectures in both domains (3.92 VMEC++, 2.58 airfoils) -- consistent with it being the architecture most prone to this failure mode in general -- but "lowest of three, still clearly healthy" is a different finding than "collapsed," and nothing here shows collapse.

**A real caveat on finding 3, worth stating plainly rather than overselling the diversity number:** this measures diversity across *all* 30 raw decodes, not filtered to the ones that would actually pass the physics oracle. For VMEC++ in particular, where only 56-70% of decodes converge at all, some of that measured spread is plausibly "wandering into invalid territory" rather than "many different, equally-good valid designs" -- the within-anchor diversity number (9.6 for cVAE) is even larger than that same model's generated-to-real distance (7.0), meaning the 30 candidates for one target spread out more from *each other* than they do from the nearest real point individually, which is at least consistent with a meaningful chunk of that spread being off-manifold excursions rather than purely useful variety. Airfoils' much higher validity rate makes its diversity number more trustworthy as "genuine variety among valid designs" as-is.

**Bottom line, in plain terms: this is not retrieval.** Nothing is copying training rows (0% near-duplicates everywhere), nothing has collapsed to a narrow safe answer (diversity clears the real-design-spacing bar everywhere, GAN included), and the domain where designs wander farthest from real data (VMEC++) is also the domain that's worse at actually landing on something valid -- a coherent, not contradictory, picture.

**Open:**
- Diversity hasn't been re-measured filtered to only oracle-verified-valid candidates -- would separate "genuine variety among good designs" from "spread that includes invalid excursions," particularly relevant for VMEC++'s numbers per the caveat above.
- Only one seed (0), one anchor set, one k (30) tested -- not swept the way the magnitude ablation was.
- Nearest-neighbor distance in raw standardized-coefficient space doesn't account for the fact that different targets may warrant genuinely different real neighborhoods -- a candidate could show a "healthy" distance from the *nearest* real row while nonetheless being closer to real designs than to anything physically sensible for its own specific requested target; a target-matched version of this check (nearest real row *within a similar target region*, not globally) would be a stronger read.

## 27. Formalizing the "Optimization Gym": a real schema, plus a triage of 7 candidate domains against it

User proposed reframing this whole project's direction: not a growing pile of physics-oracle domains, but a standardized family of environments sharing one shape (`x -> oracle(s) -> y`, validity `v(x)`, multiple fidelities), with three distinct tasks -- **target** (inverse design, what validity already measures), **navigate** (local design-geometry, what steerability already measures), and **budgeted discovery** (given a finite oracle budget spent across fidelity tiers of different cost, reach a goal as cheaply as possible -- genuinely new, nothing in this project tests it). Also proposed 7 new candidate domains (structural/Warp FEM, photonics/Meep, wind turbine/WISDEM, battery/PyBaMM, compressible CFD/JAX-Fluids, porous flow/Warp Darcy, spacecraft/Tudat) and asked, as the concrete next step, to formalize the actual schema first -- "that will make it immediately obvious which of these integrations are genuinely clean versus ones we're forcing into the framework."

**Pushback given directly, grounded in what this project has already hit, not speculation:** the proposal's "GPU: Excellent" column for several candidates (Warp, JAX-Fluids) doesn't imply harness-compatibility. TORAX is JAX-native and GPU-capable (§25) and still doesn't fit `oracle_harness.py`'s subprocess-per-candidate pattern, because its cost is dominated by a one-time JIT-compile tax, not the physics -- the same shape almost certainly applies to any other JIT-compiled framework (Warp, JAX-FEM, JAX-Fluids all compile via CUDA graphs / JAX JIT). Flagged as a real risk to carry into the schema as its own axis (harness-fit), separate from raw compute capability.

**Where the reframing is right and immediately useful**: the fidelity-ladder idea isn't hypothetical here -- VMEC++ already has one (`low`/`medium`/`high` presets) and airfoils/XFOIL explicitly doesn't (`FIDELITY_PRESETS = {"low": "xfoil"}`, flagged open in §21, never closed). The reframing retroactively surfaces a real existing gap. "Navigate" is functionally what `eval_cvae_steerability.py` already does; "budgeted discovery" is the one task type with zero existing implementation.

**Built `scripts/gym_schema.py`** -- real dataclasses, not just another prose docstring like `oracle_base.py`:
- `Candidate`, `FidelityLevel` (now carrying a `cost_credits` field oracle_base.py's plain-string `FIDELITY_PRESETS` never had), `OracleResult` (splits `ok` -- did the oracle itself run -- from a new, separate `valid` field -- does the result pass a domain-specific legality check -- deliberately NOT how either existing domain currently works: airfoil's `cd<1e-6` rejection, §22, is folded straight into `worker_fn`'s own `ok` flag today, conflating "solver didn't converge" with "solver converged but the answer is garbage"; this schema treats that as a real, if minor, existing violation worth separating out, not a description of current behavior).
- `DomainSpec` -- generalizes `oracle_base.py`'s interface into one structured object, plus the two genuinely new fields (`validity_fn`, `harness_fit`).
- Three task dataclasses (`TargetTask`, `NavigateTask`, `BudgetedDiscoveryTask`) and one common `TaskResult` (target_error, validity_rate, oracle_credits_spent, n_evaluations, diversity, pareto_improvement) -- unifies what `eval_cvae_steerability.py` (correct_direction_rate, selectivity_ratio) and `eval_random_direction_steerability.py` (cosine similarity) currently compute as separate, incompatible metric sets.
- `vmec_spec()`/`airfoil_spec()` -- real adapters wrapping the two existing domain modules, with `cost_credits` computed from this project's own already-measured wall-clock numbers (airfoil low ~30ms = 1 credit, the cheapest call in the whole project; VMEC low ~5-15s = 333 credits; VMEC medium ~48s = 1600 credits; VMEC high unmeasured, `nan`, since no run has ever actually used that preset). **Verified by import and construction, not just written**: both adapters build real `DomainSpec` objects, `worker_fn` references resolve and are callable, all four task/result dataclasses construct without error.

**Triage of the 7 proposed domains against the schema** (`CANDIDATE_TRIAGE` in the same file) -- explicitly marked as confidence-rated hypotheses, not measurements, since none of the 7 were installed or run this pass (unlike TORAX, which got a real spike): Warp FEM, JAX-Fluids, and Warp Darcy are all flagged **"needs batched worker"** on the same JIT-tax hypothesis as TORAX -- medium confidence, would need the same kind of real spike to confirm. Battery/PyBaMM stands out as the closest existing conceptual fit to the ladder idea -- it already has a *named*, real fidelity hierarchy (SPM -> SPMe -> DFN -> thermal/degradation) as part of its own design, not something this project would have to invent. WISDEM and Tudat are flagged **"likely fits"** (CPU-based, not JIT-compiled) at low-medium confidence -- plausible based on how they're built, not confirmed. Photonics/Meep is flagged **unconfirmed either way** -- FDTD is a different computational shape from anything else on the list, no basis yet for a harness-fit guess.

**Open:**
- None of the 7 candidates were actually installed, run, or license-checked this pass -- the triage table is a prioritization aid for *where to spend a real spike next*, explicitly not a substitute for one (the same "confirmed by reading source, not assumed" bar TORAX was held to).
- `validity_fn` is defined in the schema but neither `vmec_spec()` nor `airfoil_spec()` actually populates it (both pass `None`) -- realizing the ok/valid split for real would mean pulling airfoil's `cd<1e-6` check out of `worker_fn` and into a standalone `validity_fn`, not done here.
- `BudgetedDiscoveryTask` has a type but no harness loop implementing it (propose -> pick fidelity -> spend -> observe -> repeat) -- the schema makes the task nameable/serializable, not runnable yet.
- Cost credits for VMEC++'s `high` fidelity preset are unmeasured (`nan`) -- that preset has never actually been called in this project.

## 31. TORAX becomes the third real domain -- a genuine test of the abstraction, and it found a real gap

Direct user request: extend to TORAX now, "considering it a test for our abstraction." A good one -- TORAX is JAX-native, JIT-compiled, and (per §25's spike) has a fundamentally different cost profile from VMEC++/XFOIL. This section reports honestly where `gym_schema.py` held up unchanged and where it didn't.

**Resolved the harness question empirically, definitively, before building anything on top of it.** Same config, same process: **4.92s (cold) -> 0.13s (warm)**, confirmed with a real TORAX call, not assumed from §25's example run alone. More importantly, varying a REAL parameter between calls (not just rerunning identically) still only cost ~0.38-0.45s in a warm process vs. 5.1s cold -- a ~10-13x speedup even under genuine variation, not just literal repetition. This settles what §25/§27 could only hypothesize: TORAX needs a persistent-worker harness, full stop.

**Real output schema, inspected directly, not guessed.** `PostProcessedOutputs` exposes a large set of genuinely meaningful scalar tokamak physics quantities per run -- `Q_fusion` (fusion gain), `tau_E` (energy confinement time), `H98`/`H89P`/`H97L`/`H20` (confinement quality vs. various standard scalings), `T_e_volume_avg`/`T_i_volume_avg`, `beta_N`, `q95`, `f_bootstrap`, dozens more. Chose 4 for the target vector, matching this project's now-standard 4-target convention: **Q_fusion, tau_E, H98, T_e_volume_avg**.

**Design vector, per direct user choice ("scalar knobs + geometry" over scalars-only or a full custom parameterization)**: 10 continuous scalars -- Ip (plasma current), nbar (line-averaged density), chi_i/chi_e (constant-model heat diffusivity), P_total (auxiliary heating power), I_generic (generic current drive), plus 4 geometry scalars (R_major, a_minor, B_0, elongation_LCFS) on a simple circular geometry. **Every override confirmed to actually build and run correctly via `ToraxConfig.from_dict`** (not assumed from the config schema) -- tested each override group individually before writing `torax_oracle.py`, then confirmed real, distinct output values (Q_fusion 0.16-21.67, H98 0.35-2.59 across 5 random draws) once the module was written.

**Built `scripts/oracle_harness_persistent.py`** -- the genuinely new harness shape this test was expected to need. Spawns `n_workers` long-lived processes up front (each doing its own one-time imports), distributes candidates across the live pool rather than one-shot-per-candidate, and yields `(tag, ok, payload)` same as `oracle_harness.run_batch_with_timeout` -- but a per-candidate timeout here terminates and **respawns only that one worker** (losing its warm cache, paying the compile tax again on its next job) rather than treating every candidate as disposable the way the subprocess-per-candidate harness does.

**Built `scripts/torax_oracle.py`** implementing `oracle_base.py`'s interface, plus a new `persistent_worker_fn(conn)` entry point (loop over many candidates in one warm process) alongside the standard one-shot `worker_fn` (kept for interface parity, not meant for real use here). Wired into `gym_schema.py` as `torax_spec()` -- `harness_fit="needs-batched-worker"` is now a *confirmed* fact for this domain, not the hypothesis it still is for the other 7 candidates in `CANDIDATE_TRIAGE`.

**Built the new Docker image** (`Dockerfile.torax`, Python 3.12-slim + `pip install torax`, CPU-only via `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu`) and a `torax` `docker-compose.yml` service -- genuinely standalone, no shared layers with `train`/`oracle` per the numpy>=2/Python>=3.12 conflict already documented in §25.

**Validated end-to-end with real candidates, not a mock**: 5 random-parameter candidates + 1 deliberately degenerate one (`a_minor=0.001`, near-zero minor radius) through the real persistent-worker harness, `n_workers=3`. Practically: `oracle_harness_persistent.py`'s per-candidate timeout+respawn logic is a **required** safety feature for this domain, not a defensive extra -- confirmed necessary, not just prudent.

**Verdict on the abstraction test itself, the thing this section was actually for**: `DomainSpec`'s `target_names`/`param_dim`/`zero_indices`/`fidelities`/`validity_fn`/`sanity_filter`/`dataset_paths` fields all transferred to a third, meaningfully different domain with zero changes needed to the schema itself -- `torax_spec()` populates every one of them the same way `vmec_spec()`/`airfoil_spec()` do. The one real gap, exactly the one predicted in §25/§27 and not a surprise: `worker_fn`'s single-shot-subprocess contract doesn't fit a JIT-compiled oracle, needing the new `persistent_worker_fn`/`oracle_harness_persistent.py` pair alongside it. A worthwhile test: the schema mostly held, and the one place it didn't was already flagged in advance, not discovered as a surprise failure.

**Open:**
- No bulk dataset generated yet -- oracle validated in isolation (the same stage airfoils were at after §21, before §22 built on top of it). Needs a seeding strategy; stellarator data can't seed this (confirmed §29), TORAX's own bundled example configs (`basic_config`, `iterhybrid_rampup`, `iterhybrid_predictor_corrector`, `step_flattop_bgb`) are the natural candidates, untested.

## 33. Bootstrap candidate/data search, made real: a target-space coverage metric, and a first live run

Direct follow-up to the user's own framing: "we want to get the... bootstrapping candidate/data search set up... and we have to come up with some kind of success metric... basically like having good coverage over broader sections of target space." `bootstrap_generic.py` (§28) existed but was only smoke-tested (12/24 airfoil, 7/8 VMEC); this section runs it for real, builds the coverage metric the user asked for, and uses it to find something actionable.

**A real bug, caught by the metric itself, not by inspection.** The very first coverage run produced a nonsense `mean_reach_ratio` of 76x. Traced directly: `run_bootstrap`'s acceptance gate only checked "the oracle ran and returned finite numbers" -- never the domain's own `sanity_filter` (airfoil's `cd>=1e-6`/`|l_over_d|<=300`, §22) that every training script applies. A handful of accepted candidates had `l_over_d` up to **61,069** (near-zero-but-not-below-threshold `cd`, the same numerically-unstable-XFOIL-output failure mode §22 originally built the filter to catch, just never wired into this newer script). Fixed by threading `spec.sanity_filter` through `run_bootstrap`'s accept/reject gate (a new `failed_sanity_filter` rejection reason, same bookkeeping as every other reject path). Hit rate barely moved once fixed (73.2%->73.1% airfoil anchor) -- confirms the bad candidates were rare, not a large fraction, but they were exactly the ones that would have silently wrecked any coverage number downstream.

**Built the coverage metric (`scripts/coverage_metric.py`), two numbers not one, since "coverage" has two different failure modes:**
- **FILL** -- does generation reach into regions that are already possible but *under-represented* in the real dataset (gaps inside the known range)? Directly generalizes this project's own §9 VMEC++ finding ("93.9% of generated designs landed nearest an under-covered cluster, vs. ~50% baseline") into a reusable, domain-agnostic metric instead of a one-off hand-computed number: clusters below the real data's own median size are "under-covered"; `fill_lift` = generated pool's hit-rate on those clusters, divided by the real data's own share landing there (the correct null -- not a flat 50%, computed directly since cluster sizes aren't uniform).
- **REACH** -- does generation extend *beyond* the real dataset's existing convex range, not just fill gaps inside it? Projects real data and the generated pool onto random unit directions in target-z-space (the same `sample_unit_directions` mechanism `steerability_generic.py`/`bootstrap_generic.py` already use to evaluate/drive direction-following) and compares max projections; the null is a same-size random resample of the real data itself (needed because max-of-N grows with N even from a fixed distribution -- a naive "did we beat the real max" check would be biased by generated-pool size alone).

**Needed real target-space cluster infrastructure to compute FILL, which didn't exist generically yet.** `make_splits.py` already had a VMEC-only `target-cluster` mode (k-means over z-scored, log-transformed targets, same idea as its `cluster` mode but on Y instead of X) -- generalized it into `make_splits_generic.py` via a new `--space {input, target}` flag (the old default is now `--space input`, unchanged behavior), reusing the same `kmeans`/`greedy_group_assign` and saving `cluster_centroids.npy` (needed to assign *new* generated points to the real data's own clusters, which the split-only version never had to do). Built for both domains: airfoil (30 clusters, sizes 662-2,313) and VMEC++ (20 clusters, sizes 1,501-15,630) -- no empty clusters either way.

**First real (non-smoke) bootstrap runs, GAN generator (this project's consistently strongest steerer, §23/§30), fixed acceptance gate:**

| domain | seed-mode | step_std | target_count | accepted/attempted | hit rate | wall time |
|---|---|--:|--:|---|--:|--:|
| airfoil | anchor | 1.5 (default) | 5,000 | 5,073/6,944 | 73.1% | 107s |
| airfoil | prior | -- | 5,000 | 5,002/8,400 | 59.5% | 141s |
| airfoil | anchor | 4.0 | 3,000 | 3,035/7,056 | 43.0% | 120s |
| vmec | anchor | 1.5 (default) | 100 | 161/336 | 47.9% | 269s |

VMEC's own throughput gap vs. airfoil (161 accepted in 269s vs. thousands/2min for airfoil) is the same oracle-cost gap this project has documented since §21 -- not a new finding, just visible again here at bootstrap scale.

**Coverage results:**

| pool | fill_lift (vs. null) | clusters hit | mean reach ratio | frac. directions exceeding real max (vs. null) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| airfoil anchor (step 1.5) | 1.31x | 30/30 | 0.79 | 24.5% (null: 0%) |
| airfoil prior | 1.36x | 30/30 | 0.83 | 24.5% (null: 0%) |
| **airfoil anchor (step 4.0)** | **1.96x** | 30/30 | **1.03** | **60.5%** (null: 0%) |
| vmec anchor (step 1.5) | 1.02x | 17/20 | 0.84 | 28.5% (null: 0%) |

**A real, honest, non-uniform result -- coverage isn't automatically good just because the generator is target-conditioned.** At the default `step_std=1.5`, airfoil's bootstrap pool shows genuine FILL signal (31-36% more likely to land in an under-covered cluster than the real data's own distribution would predict) but its REACH is actually *worse* than a same-size random resample of the real data (0.79-0.83 vs. the resample null's own 0.90) -- the generator is staying safely inside the known range, not just failing to explore, actively pulled inward relative to chance. **`step_std` is a real, well-behaved dial for this**: pushing it from 1.5 to 4.0 nearly doubles the FILL lift (1.31x -> 1.96x) and flips REACH from below-null to clearly above both the real max and the null (1.03 vs. 0.80), at a real, expected cost (hit rate 73.1% -> 43.0%, more of the push lands the generator outside what it can turn into a still-valid design). VMEC's small-sample run shows the opposite profile: FILL is essentially flat (1.02x, likely just noise at n_gen=161) but REACH is comparatively strong (0.84 vs. a null of only 0.52, since a random resample of 161 real points barely reaches anywhere) -- not yet enough data to know if this is a real cross-domain difference or an artifact of VMEC's much smaller bootstrap sample so far.

**Open:**
- VMEC's bootstrap sample (161) is far too small to trust its coverage numbers the way airfoil's (5,000+) can be -- needs a real-scale run once remote (AICR) compute is available; not attempted at airfoil-comparable scale here given VMEC++'s per-call cost.
- Only GAN tested as the generator -- diffusion (this project's other consistently strong steerer) and cVAE not yet run through the coverage metric; unknown whether the step_std/FILL/REACH tradeoff looks the same for a different architecture.
- No search over step_std beyond the two points tested (1.5, 4.0) -- the real shape of the hit-rate/coverage tradeoff curve (is it linear? is there a cliff?) isn't characterized.
- Retraining-in-the-loop is still explicitly out of scope per the user's own prior instruction ("we can not include retraining to start") -- this section's pools are a one-shot generation from a fixed checkpoint, not fed back into anything yet.
- FILL's under-covered threshold (below-median cluster size) and REACH's direction count (200) are both reasonable defaults, not tuned or sensitivity-tested.

## 34. Coverage across all 3 architectures: a real, counter-intuitive divergence from the steerability ranking

Direct follow-up closing §33's own flagged gap ("only GAN tested as the generator"). Ran the identical anchor-mode, `step_std=1.5`, `target_count=5000` bootstrap through `coverage_metric.py` for diffusion and cVAE too (airfoil, the domain with real scale to trust this at):

|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| GAN | 73.1% | 1.31x | 0.79 | 24.5% |
| Diffusion | 69.6% | 1.37x | **0.99** | **48.5%** |
| cVAE | 53.5% | **1.48x** | 0.91 | 35.5% |

**The coverage ranking is not the steerability ranking -- it's closer to inverted.** Every eval this project has run since §18 (validity, correct-direction rate, selectivity, arbitrary-direction cosine) ranks these three GAN > diffusion > cVAE, consistently, across both domains. Coverage flips that: **cVAE has the best FILL** (1.48x, clearly ahead of both others) and **diffusion has the best REACH** (0.99, essentially matching the real data's own max and far above GAN's 0.79) while **GAN -- the strongest steerer -- has the worst REACH of the three**, actually below the do-nothing resample null.

A plausible mechanism, not yet confirmed: the same property that makes GAN the most precise, confident steerer (§18-30's own finding -- GAN "knows" the requested direction even for small asks, §19) may be exactly what keeps it conservative here -- a generator that's learned to produce sharp, high-confidence outputs close to what the training manifold actually looks like has less reason to wander into genuinely novel target-space territory. cVAE's own comparative *weakness* as a steerer (blurrier, lower-confidence conditioning, the same property that made it this project's weakest architecture on every other test) may be exactly what lets it reach into more varied regions almost by accident. This is a real, if speculative, "the metric you optimize is the metric you get" story -- not confirmed mechanistically, flagged as the natural next thing to actually test (e.g. does artificially adding decode-time noise to the GAN/diffusion samplers recover some of the coverage gap, without an architecture change) rather than assumed.

**Practical implication for actually using this in a real bootstrap pipeline**: no single architecture is uniformly "best" for growing a dataset -- GAN if the immediate goal is oracle-hit-rate-efficient generation near the known manifold (73.1% hit rate, cheapest per accepted design), cVAE or diffusion if the goal is genuinely expanding target-space coverage, at a real, honest cost in hit rate (53.5%/69.6%). A production bootstrap loop wanting both might reasonably mix architectures per round rather than picking one, though that's a proposal, not something tested here.

**Open:**
- Mechanism (confidence/sharpness driving the coverage/steerability trade) is a hypothesis, not confirmed -- no controlled test isolating it yet.
- Not checked whether this pattern holds at the higher step_std=4.0 setting (§33's own finding that step_std trades hit-rate for coverage) for diffusion/cVAE too, or is GAN-specific.

## 38. Chasing §37's diffusion convergence gap to a real mechanism: catastrophic extrapolation into structurally-invalid geometry, not a numerical fluke

| model | converged | failures violating a sanity cap | median R_major, converged/failed | median elongation, converged/failed | median Ip, converged/failed |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| cVAE | 97.1% (233/240) | 0/7 (0%) | 7.24 / 5.78 | 1.75 / 1.72 | 1.39e7 / 1.46e7 |
| Diffusion | 47.5% (114/240) | 100/126 (**79.4%**) | 5.04 / **170** | 1.49 / **-15** | 1.01e7 / **4.09e8** |

**This is a real, clean mechanism, not a numerical fluke.** cVAE's rare failures (7/240) look geometrically indistinguishable from its successes -- it essentially never generates wild geometry even when pushed, consistent with its comparatively muted directional signal (cosine ~0.6, §37). **Plausible architectural explanation, flagged as a hypothesis, not confirmed mechanistically:** a GAN's generator is trained under direct adversarial pressure from a critic that has specifically learned to recognize implausible (fake-looking) outputs -- a built-in force discouraging wildly-off-manifold generations under any conditioning, including an unfamiliar pushed target. Diffusion's denoising-score-matching objective and cVAE's KL-toward-a-smooth-Gaussian-prior objective both lack that specific "does this look real" pressure; cVAE's own smoothing (§18-20's whole "cVAE is the blurriest, least confident architecture" pattern, its comparatively weak steering signal here too) may substitute a different kind of implicit regularization (regression toward the training mean under uncertain conditioning) that happens to keep it safe here, while diffusion's iterative denoising process has no equivalent found in this project's evidence to point to and, per this result, extrapolates far more boldly (and precisely, when it lands validly) but with no guardrail against overshooting into physically impossible territory. This double-explains both halves of §37's original finding: diffusion's convergent candidates are the MOST accurately steered because it commits fully to the requested direction rather than blending conservatively, and its convergence is the WORST for exactly the same reason -- committing fully sometimes means committing to a nonphysical design.

**Practical implication**: none of this project's 3 generative architectures fail uniformly across domains and settings, another instance of the "no single best architecture" pattern from §34/§37 -- but for TORAX specifically, diffusion's failure mode is now understood well enough that a targeted mitigation is plausible (e.g. clipping/rejecting decoded params against the input-side sanity caps before even calling the oracle, cheap since it's a pure post-decode check) -- not built here, flagged as a natural next step.

- The adversarial-pressure explanation is plausible, not tested -- would need e.g. a controlled ablation (a diffusion variant with an added realism/regularization term) to actually confirm, not attempted this pass.

## 45. jax-cfd's design parameterization, resolved for real: wall BCs are unimplemented, a fully-periodic penalized nozzle works and passes its physical-sanity check

Direct follow-up to §44's open item, and a real user question first ("does jax_cfd want a mesh instead of a parameterization?") that surfaced the right framing: jax_cfd has no immersed-obstacle primitive at all, mesh or otherwise -- confirmed once more while chasing this section's own dead end below. User picked the nozzle/duct-wall-shape direction (§44's option 1) to spike for real.

**§44's `channel_flow_boundary_conditions` turned out to be unimplemented in the actual solver, not just untested -- confirmed three independent ways, not one guess:**
1. The default advection schemes (`advect_van_leer`/`advect_linear`/`advect_upwind`, all routed through `advect_general`) contain an explicit guard: `if not boundaries.has_all_periodic_boundary_conditions(c): raise NotImplementedError(...)`.
2. Swapping in `convect_linear` (a separate code path that doesn't call `advect_general`) instead crashed with an internal `ValueError: the GridArray does not contain all interior grid values` deep in `boundaries.py`'s own offset-alignment bookkeeping, using `HomogeneousBoundaryConditions`.
3. Retried with explicit `ConstantBoundaryConditions` (real Dirichlet zero values instead of the homogeneous default) instead of chasing (2) further -- hit a *different*, cleaner, explicit guard: `get_advection_flux_bc_from_velocity_and_scalar` itself raises `NotImplementedError('Flux boundary condition is not implemented for velocity with ConstantBoundaryConditions(...dirichlet...))`.

Not chased into a jax_cfd source patch -- treated as a hard library limitation to design around, the same way §21 treated XFOIL's own missing OpenFOAM tier as a real gap rather than something to fix upstream.

**Worked around by never invoking the broken code path at all**, not by fixing it: fully **periodic** domain in both dimensions (the one BC type §44 already proved works end-to-end), with the actual duct -- both the constant-width outer pipe walls *and* the converging-diverging nozzle constriction -- represented entirely as a **Brinkman-penalization body force** (`-mask(x,y) * velocity / epsilon`, epsilon=2e-3) added to a constant streamwise driving force, rather than as any kind of geometric/BC boundary. `x` = 1-2 Fourier coefficients on `cos((k+1) * 2*pi*x/LX)`, added to a fixed base half-height -- inherently periodicity-consistent (no seam discontinuity at x=0/LX by construction, unlike a boundary-fitted mesh would need to enforce by hand).

**A real coordinate-order bug caught by the sanity check itself, not by inspection**: first run gave fluid_frac 0.12-0.21 (way too little open channel for a nominal half-height of 0.42, i.e. ~84% should be fluid) and a *non-monotonic* bulk-velocity response to constriction tightening -- traced to `grid.mesh()` returning `(x_mesh, y_mesh)` in that order, but the mask-building code had unpacked it as `ys, xs = grid.mesh(...)`, silently swapping which axis the constriction narrowed. Confirmed the correct order directly (`grid.mesh()[0].max() == LX`, `[1].max() == LY`) before fixing, the same "verify, don't assume" bar §21/§30 already held other axis/shape bugs to.

| constriction | fluid area fraction | bulk mean u | NaN? |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| none | 0.844 | 2.934 | no |
| mild | 0.825 | 2.750 | no |
| tight | 0.739 | 1.277 | no |

Monotonically decreasing (confirmed programmatically, not eyeballed) -- a narrower throat produces measurably more resistance and less throughput for the same driving force, exactly the physical direction a real constriction should move it, and a large, clearly-outside-noise effect (2.9 -> 1.3, more than 2x) rather than a marginal one. `fluid_frac` for the unconstricted case (0.844) also lands almost exactly on the analytically-expected value (2 x 0.42 / 1.0 = 0.84) -- a second, independent confirmation the geometry is being built correctly, not just that the physics trend happens to point the right way. Fast: ~0.24s warm per candidate at 128x64 on GPU (first call ~0.93s, compile tax, consistent with §44's own vmap-batching numbers).

**Reads as: a real, working `x -> oracle -> y` shape now exists for this domain** -- `x` = Fourier coefficients on the constriction profile, `y` = bulk throughput (and, not yet extracted, throat velocity / pressure drop / separation indicators from the same run) -- closing §44's one open item that every other domain here didn't have to solve. ## 46. A 5th domain, chosen for the opposite reason §44 picked jax-cfd: deliberately CPU-only, self-written, intuitive -- insulated-mug thermal design spiked and passing its own sanity checks

**Physics**: transient heat conduction through a mug/thermos wall, not a wrapped external simulator -- `scripts/thermal_mug_spike.py` implements a 1D (thin-wall/planar approximation, since wall thickness is small relative to a ~3.5cm mug radius) finite-volume conduction model, implicit (backward Euler, unconditionally stable, solved via `scipy.linalg.solve_banded` every step), liquid as a lumped-capacitance node, ambient as a fixed-temperature boundary. `x` = `[t_wall_mm, t_gap_mm, k_gap]` -- structural (steel) shell thickness, insulation-layer thickness, and insulation conductivity as a continuous stand-in for material choice (vacuum-like ~0.005 .. foam/air ~0.03 .. poor ~0.05 W/(m K), real order-of-magnitude values). `y` = `retention_time_s` (time to cool from 90C to 60C, `None`/flagged if not reached rather than silently clipped -- same convention as every other domain's non-convergence signal), `mass_kg` (material cost/weight proxy), `touch_temp_60s_C` (outer-wall safety temperature 60s after filling).

**Verified the FD implementation against a closed-form limit before trusting it** (same bar as §21's NACA0012 check, §45's analytic fluid_frac check): with wall/gap thermal mass scaled to ~0, the FD solution should collapse to a simple lumped-RC exponential decay with a closed-form time constant. Confirmed to **0.01-0.03% relative error** across two parameter sets -- the numerical scheme itself is correct, independent of how well the underlying physical model matches a real mug.

**A real discretization bug caught by a sanity check, not by inspection** (same pattern as §45's `grid.mesh()` axis swap, §33's sanity-filter gap): first pass at 15 fixed nodes per layer gave a *non-monotonic* `touch_temp_60s_C` at large `t_gap` (41.87C at 10mm, ticking back up to 43.44C at 20mm) -- physically wrong, since more insulation should only ever slow the outer surface's early-time warming further. Fixed by raising `N_GAP` 15->40 (cost: 37.8ms -> 40.3ms/candidate, negligible) -- monotonicity restored (40.50C at 10mm vs. 40.75C at 20mm, the residual near-flatness at large `t_gap` reads as a real physical plateau, not a bug: past a certain thickness the 60s outer-surface reading is governed by local near-surface diffusion into what's effectively a semi-infinite slab, not total thickness).

**Sanity checks pass, both sweeps monotonic in the physically correct direction**: (1) fixed material, sweeping insulation thickness 0/2/5/10mm -- retention 74.6/122.8/195.2/316.4 min, touch-temp 87.4/61.4/46.7/40.5C, both moving the right way, a large (>4x) and clearly-outside-discretization-noise effect; (2) fixed thickness, sweeping insulation quality (k_gap 0.05->0.005, poor->vacuum-like) -- retention 147/214/316 min -> not reached in 6h, touch-temp 54.9->44.5->37.5->29.8C, same direction, same magnitude of effect.

This is a real two-axis design tension in the spirit of airfoil's `cl`/`cd`/`cm` or VMEC's `aspect_ratio`/`elongation`/`qi`: a good design isn't simply "add more material," it's a genuine combination of *how much* and *what* insulation -- exactly the kind of non-trivial manifold a generative-architecture comparison needs to be measuring something real (§18-20's earlier point about degenerate design spaces applies in reverse here: this one isn't degenerate).

**Speed**: ~40ms/candidate on host CPU, single-threaded, no batching attempted yet -- already cheaper than TORAX's 0.36s/candidate and VMEC++'s multi-second calls, in the same rough range as XFOIL. ## 47. Mug domain productionized: oracle module, DomainSpec, and a real 20K-row dataset -- and a real censoring bug caught by the hit-rate check before it silently biased the data

Direct continuation of §46, closing its "spike only" gap the same way §31 built on top of TORAX's §25 spike. Built `mug_oracle.py` (`oracle_base.py` contract, wrapping `thermal_mug_spike.simulate()` unchanged -- no new physics), `mug_spec()` in `gym_schema.py` (4th real `DomainSpec` after VMEC++/airfoil/TORAX), and `generate_mug_dataset.py`.

**A real methodology bug caught by the hit-rate check itself, not by inspection** (same pattern as §33's sanity-filter gap, §45's axis swap): a first 20-candidate smoke test using §46's original `retention_time_s` target (time to cool to 60C) gave only a **20% hit rate** -- and every single rejection was "still above 60C" at the 6-hour simulated cutoff, not an actual instability. That's a silent bias, not a failure signal: it was throwing away the BEST designs (the ones insulating well enough to still be hot at 6h), the opposite of every other domain's non-convergence rejection (which discards broken candidates, not good ones). Root cause: a threshold-crossing TIME is open-ended and needs an arbitrary cutoff; a genuinely excellent thermos can legitimately take many hours to cross 60C (that's the whole point of a vacuum flask), so "not reached in 6h" conflates "broken" with "too good to measure inside this window."

**Fixed by changing the primary target's definition, not by extending the cutoff further** (that would only push the same problem out, not remove it): switched to **liquid temperature at a fixed 2-hour mark** (`temp_at_2h_C`) -- `simulate()`'s own `final_liq_temp_C` at `t_max=7200` already computes exactly this, always well-defined for any stable input. This domain's validity profile is now genuinely different from every wrapped-solver domain here: `ok=False` can basically only mean a structurally invalid input (non-positive thickness/conductivity), not a physics failure -- there's no non-convergence mode to have, since backward Euler is unconditionally stable. Worth flagging plainly as a real contrast, not assumed away.

**Correlation structure on the real 20K-row dataset, computed directly rather than trusted from the 300-sample spike**: `corr(mass_kg, temp_at_2h_C)=0.085`, `corr(mass_kg, touch_temp_60s_C)=-0.010` -- confirms §46's "mass nearly orthogonal to performance" finding at 65x the sample size. The REAL thermal tension lives entirely in the other pair: `corr(t_gap_mm, temp_at_2h_C)=0.690`, `corr(k_gap, temp_at_2h_C)=-0.544`, both with matching-sign effects on `touch_temp_60s_C` (-0.432, +0.584). **Read together: this domain has two nearly-separable axes, not one three-way tangle** -- a manufacturing/durability choice (`t_wall_mm`, drives cost, thermally near-irrelevant) and a thermal-design choice (`t_gap_mm`/`k_gap`, drives performance, doesn't move cost much). That's arguably a more realistic and more interesting structure than "more insulation costs more" would have been -- it means a designer can pick wall thickness for durability/dishwasher-safety reasons for free, and separately optimize insulation for thermal performance, matching how real product design actually decouples those two decisions. `temp_at_2h_C`/`touch_temp_60s_C` correlate at -0.930 (strong but not degenerate, ~14% independent variance -- the two targets aren't redundant, consistent with §46's transient-lag reasoning for why touch-safety was worth measuring separately).

## 48. Mug domain, v2: richer parameterization (named materials, a wall profile, a real handle) per direct user request -- three near-separable design axes found, not assumed

User pushed back on §47's 3-scalar design vector directly: wanted named materials (not one free-floating conductivity), a wall PROFILE (not a constant thickness), and explicitly asked for two new concerns -- weight ("wrist strain") and a handle that "shouldn't make the handle hot." All three are genuine new physics, not cosmetic relabeling -- built as `simulate_v2()` in `thermal_mug_spike.py`, v1's `simulate()` left untouched for reference.

**Materials**: three palettes (`STRUCTURAL_MATERIALS`, `INSULATION_MATERIALS`, `HANDLE_MATERIALS`), each a short list of real named substances (plastic/glass/ceramic/steel; vacuum/aerogel/air_gap/foam; silicone_rubber/wood/plastic_grip/ceramic/steel) with real order-of-magnitude (k, rho, cp) triples, sorted by conductivity. Each material "slot" in the design vector is a continuous index that linearly interpolates (k, rho, cp) between adjacent table entries -- keeps the parameterization fully continuous (generative-model-friendly, same style as v1's free-floating `k_gap`) while every point traces through real, nameable materials rather than an arbitrary number.

**Wall profile**: kept to 2 axial bands (rim, base) rather than a full height-resolved field -- real mugs often ARE thinner at the rim (comfortable drinking edge) and thicker at the base (stability/drop resistance), and 2 bands captures that without a 2D solve. Each band is its own independent 1D radial+gap conduction chain, splitting the cylindrical side-wall area evenly, both drawing from the same liquid.

**A real coupling-scheme decision, checked before trusting it**: v1 solved liquid+wall as ONE fully-implicit system (liquid as an unknown in the same tridiagonal solve). With 3 independent chains now needing to touch the liquid/body (rim, base, handle), that would require a non-tridiagonal star-shaped matrix. Used explicit-implicit splitting instead -- the liquid's own energy balance updates EXPLICITLY each step from the previous step's band fluxes, each chain (rim/base/handle) solves fully implicitly using the previous step's liquid/body temperature as a fixed boundary. **Verified this is still consistent with v1's own already-verified scheme, not assumed**: reduced to v1's exact case (equal bands, steel/foam) -- `final_liq_temp_C` 69.65C (v1) vs. 69.97C (v2, 0.46% relative), `touch_temp_60s_C` 46.67C vs. 46.68C. Close enough to trust the new scheme without re-deriving its own closed-form check.

**Handle: a genuine transient fin-conduction model, a quasi-steady shortcut explicitly checked and rejected first.** A quasi-steady approach (steady-state fin formula at each instant) implicitly assumes the handle's own thermal diffusion time is short vs. the 60s reporting window -- checked directly: `tau ~ L^2/alpha` for a ~5cm steel handle is ~667s, and for a low-conductivity handle (precisely the "safe" designs this target exists to identify) the ratio is no better -- neither clears 60s, so the transient had to be solved for real, not shortcut. Implemented as a 1D fin: implicit backward-Euler chain along the handle's length WITH a lateral convective-loss term at every cell (the standard extended-surface/fin equation term v1's wall chains never needed, since those only lose heat at their two ends), Dirichlet-coupled at the base to the previous step's average of the two wall bands' outer surface temperatures, adiabatic (zero-flux) tip (standard for a fin whose tip area is small vs. its lateral area). Starts at ambient, not fill temperature, unlike the wall -- physically correct, since the handle is remote from the liquid and only heats via conduction from the body.

**A real, non-obvious physical finding caught by a monotonicity sweep, not assumed away**: sweeping `HANDLE_MATERIALS` by conductivity at fixed length/diameter, `wood` (k=0.17) came out HOTTER than `plastic_grip` (k=0.20) at 60s -- backwards from "higher k = hotter" intuition. Traced to thermal diffusivity, not conductivity alone, governing short-time transient response: `alpha = k/(rho*cp)` -- wood's alpha (1.67e-7 m2/s) is actually higher than plastic's (1.108e-7) because wood's volumetric heat capacity (`rho*cp`) is lower, so despite lower k, wood's thermal front advances faster in the first 60 seconds. Real physics, not a bug -- confirmed by direct calculation, not dismissed -- and a genuine reason this domain's material index isn't simply "monotonic in k" for early-transient targets, worth remembering before assuming a generative model's material-steering behavior "should" be monotonic either.

**A second real finding from the same sweep, also checked rather than assumed**: at realistic handle lengths (>=~20mm) and 60s, every non-steel material sits at essentially ambient temperature (only `steel`, by far the highest-diffusivity material in the table, shows meaningful warming) -- checked out to 600s (10 min) too, same pattern holds. This is a genuine, physically correct result (it's *why* rubber/wood/plastic grips exist -- they really don't transmit body heat that far in that little time), not a data-generation flaw, but it does mean `handle_temp_60s_C` is heavily floor-dominated across the sampled space -- confirmed at real dataset scale below, not just the sweep.

**Real dataset regenerated for the new 8-dim design vector** (`generate_mug_dataset.py` updated for direct range sampling over all 8 params -- still no archetypal seeds needed, confirmed again by a 40/40 smoke test): **20,000 rows, 100% hit rate, 344s** (v2's ~227ms/candidate, ~5x v1's ~40ms given 3 coupled chains instead of 1, but still cheap and 100%-converging by construction). `handle_temp_60s_C`: **76.0% of rows sit within 0.1C of ambient** -- the floor-dominated shape predicted by the sweep, confirmed at scale, not just anecdote.

**The real payoff -- three largely separable design axes, checked via correlation, not assumed from the sweeps alone:**

| driver | temp_at_2h_C | mass_kg | touch_temp_60s_C | handle_temp_60s_C |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| t_gap_mm | 0.789 | 0.068 | -0.537 | -0.009 |
| insulation_material_idx | -0.347 | -0.037 | 0.214 | 0.012 |

Reads cleanly as three near-independent knobs: **(1) thermal performance** (`t_gap_mm`/`insulation_material_idx` drive `temp_at_2h_C`/`touch_temp_60s_C`, nothing else does), **(2) structural cost/weight** (`t_wall_rim/base_mm`/`struct_material_idx` drive `mass_kg` almost exclusively -- `struct_material_idx` alone at 0.684 is the single strongest correlation in the whole table, steel vs. plastic's ~8x density difference dominating), **(3) handle safety** (`handle_length_mm`/`handle_material_idx` drive `handle_temp_60s_C` and nothing else, both roughly balanced in magnitude and correctly signed). Cross-target correlations confirm the same story from the other direction: `mass_kg` vs. every safety/performance target is ~0 (0.007-0.107), `handle_temp_60s_C` vs. everything except its own two drivers is ~0 (-0.031 to 0.062). This is a materially richer structure than §47's two-axis finding -- a real design here trades off across three genuinely different concerns, matching the "GAN/VAE numbers would mean something real" bar this whole investigation started from, now with more to be selective about (a domain where selectivity -- does nudging one target avoid dragging the others -- has three, not two, largely-independent things to not drag).

## 50. Mug domain, v3: user asked to "uplevel" it further -- picked a lid/top-loss model and a variable body shape from a 4-option menu, both real physics gaps, not cosmetic additions

Direct user request, offered 4 candidate directions with an honest ranking (lid/top-loss flagged as highest-value since v1/v2 had a real correctness gap, not just a missing nice-to-have -- the top of the mug lost ZERO heat, backwards from reality where an open liquid surface is typically a FASTER loss pathway than an insulated side wall). User picked lid/top-loss + variable body shape, deferred richer-handle and hot/cold-dual-mode. Built `simulate_v3()` in `thermal_mug_spike.py` (v2's `simulate_v2()` untouched for reference).

**Body shape**: radius at 3 heights (base/mid/rim) instead of one fixed cylinder radius -- taper, flare, and belly shapes are all expressible now (straight cylinder is just the r_base=r_mid=r_rim special case). Each half-height band is now a real frustum (truncated cone), not a plain cylinder shell -- lateral area (`frustum_lateral_area`) and liquid volume (`frustum_volume`) both computed from the actual geometry per candidate, not the fixed module-level constants v1/v2 used. Height stays fixed (not varied this pass -- flagged as a further idea, not done, to keep this pass's parameter growth bounded).

**Lid**: one continuous `lid_coverage_frac` in [0,1] interpolates open-cup (0) to fully-sealed (1) -- avoids a discrete branch, matching this domain's continuous-parameterization style everywhere else. The open fraction of the top loses heat via a temperature-DEPENDENT effective coefficient (`H_AIR_TOP_OPEN=15` baseline + `EVAP_COEFF=60` scaled by how far above ambient the liquid still is) -- an explicit, documented stand-in for evaporation (which really does scale with vapor pressure, which really does rise with temperature) rather than a full Antoine-equation model; chosen because the ballpark magnitude (open coffee cools much faster than lidded -- common experience) matters more here than the exact curve shape. The covered fraction conducts through a lid (own thickness + a material index reusing `STRUCTURAL_MATERIALS`, not a 4th materials table) to ambient at the plain convection coefficient.

**A real initial-condition bug caught by the coverage sweep itself, not by inspection** (same pattern as every prior domain bug here): first pass initialized the lid at fill temperature (`T0_LIQUID`), matching the wall bands' own convention. Result: `touch_temp_at_C` pinned near 90C at every coverage level >0 for the entire 60s window, regardless of lid thickness/material/coverage fraction -- a dead giveaway once compared against the coverage=0 case (46.55C, sane). Root cause: a lid isn't liquid-wetted at t=0 the way a submerged wall shell is (the wall-band convention is itself a stated fill-event approximation, §46) -- it only heats via conduction from below, so it should start at ambient, exactly like the handle already does. Fixed (`T_lid = np.full(N_LID, T_AMB)`) -- re-verified clean afterward.

**Verification, since a numeric regression match against v2 isn't the right bar here** (v2's assumption -- zero top loss -- is itself unphysical, not a baseline worth reproducing exactly): checked that v3 with a fully-sealed, thin realistic lid runs COOLER than v2's idealized zero-loss case (62.26C vs. 69.97C, correct direction -- a real lid still conducts some heat, v2 assumed none) while `touch_temp_at_C` stays close (46.44C vs. 46.68C, sensible since a thin lid barely warms in 60s). Coverage sweep (0/0.25/0.5/0.75/1.0): `temp_at_2h_C` rises monotonically (42.72->62.19C) -- more sealing, better retention, as expected; `touch_temp_at_C` jumps from 46.55C (no lid) to a roughly flat ~70.5-70.9C the instant any lid exists, independent of how MUCH area it covers -- confirmed this is real, not a bug: lid surface temperature is an intensive (per-unit-area) quantity, driven by material/thickness/liquid-temp only, genuinely independent of total covered area. Belly sweep (r_base=r_rim=35mm fixed, r_mid swept 25->60mm): liquid volume (0.285->0.725 L) and mass (0.211->0.327 kg) both rise as expected, and `temp_at_2h_C` also rises (44.82->59.26C) -- a bigger vessel at the same wall/insulation spec retains heat better per unit volume (surface-to-volume scaling, the same physical principle behind why larger animals retain body heat more efficiently), a real and previously-untestable prediction now confirmed. Speed: ~254ms/candidate (up from v2's ~215ms, the extra lid chain + top-loss term), still cheap.

**Real dataset regenerated for the 14-dim design vector** (`mug_oracle.py`/`generate_mug_dataset.py` updated; still no archetypal seeds needed, 40/40 smoke test): **20,000 rows, 100% hit rate, 412s.**

**The payoff -- correlation analysis shows the domain got MEANINGFULLY richer, not just bigger, and cross-target coupling actually DROPPED**, checked directly rather than assumed from the sweeps:

| r_rim_mm | **-0.590** | 0.248 | -0.011 | -0.015 |
| lid_coverage_frac | **0.491** | 0.130 | -0.012 | -0.010 |

**`temp_at_2h_C` is now driven by FIVE interacting parameters** (rim radius/top exposure, lid coverage, belly volume, gap thickness, insulation material) instead of v2's two dominant ones (`t_gap_mm` alone was 0.789 in v2, now only 0.284 in v3) -- correctly reflecting that once an open-top loss pathway actually exists, side-wall insulation stops being the whole story, same as real life (an open mug's wall insulation barely matters if most heat leaves through the top). **`touch_temp_60s_C` is now almost entirely a LID story** (`t_lid_mm` at -0.904, by far the strongest single correlation in the whole table) -- completely decoupled from the wall/insulation parameters that drove it in v2 (all near zero here). **Cross-target correlation dropped sharply**: `temp_at_2h_C` vs. `touch_temp_60s_C` went from -0.836 (v2) to **-0.059** (v3) -- these were coupled in v2 because both were driven by the same `t_gap`/insulation combo; in v3 they're driven by almost entirely disjoint parameter sets (retention cares about rim/lid-coverage/body-volume/gap, safety cares about lid-thickness/material), so they can now be optimized nearly independently. **Body size now genuinely costs material** (`r_mid`/`r_rim` correlate 0.22-0.25 with `mass_kg`, impossible in v2 where geometry was fixed) -- a real, previously-unexpressable design fact (a bigger mug costs more) now captured.
